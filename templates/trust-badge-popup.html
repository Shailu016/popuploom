Free trust badge popup template

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 17 November 2026 Last updated 17 November 2026 Template checked 17 November 2026

A trust badge popup puts the answers to pre-purchase doubt next to the decision. The Secure Checkout template is a bottom-left panel with a short heading, one plain-language fact and three links: returns, privacy and a human contact. It is a layout for claims you can evidence, not a badge that creates trust by itself.

This page covers the difference between a badge and a claim, the evidence to gather before publishing, where the panel belongs in a checkout path, and the phrases that turn reassurance into a liability. It is one of the trust templates.

A popup cannot certify your store. It does not process payments, audit your checkout, grant PCI compliance or verify a guarantee. Every statement in the panel is the site owner's claim and must be true, current and linked to the full policy.

What this template is for

The visitor is interested but hesitating. The reasons are usually ordinary: how long delivery takes, whether returns are easy, who answers support, and whether the payment step is handled by a known provider. The panel answers one of those questions and links to the rest.

  • A returns or guarantee summary with a link to the full policy.
  • A payment explanation naming the provider and the fact that card details are entered on its page.
  • A support route with real hours and a response expectation.
  • A delivery fact that is true today and can be updated when it changes.

It is not a substitute for a clear product page, a visible price or a working checkout. Trust copy cannot fix a store that hides shipping costs until the last step.

A badge is not a claim

A badge is a graphic. A claim is a sentence a customer can rely on. If the panel says “secure checkout”, the visitor is entitled to ask what makes it secure and who is responsible. The template is deliberately built around a sentence plus links, so the answer is one tap away.

Before publishing, write down the evidence for each statement: the policy page, the provider's documentation, the date it was checked and who owns it. If you cannot produce that evidence in five minutes, remove the sentence rather than soften it.

Use it when

  • The store has a real return, guarantee or support policy with a public page.
  • The payment provider is named in the checkout and in the privacy notice.
  • Delivery expectations are accurate for the current week.
  • Someone can update the panel when a policy changes.

Do not use it to imply a certification, an audit, a payment-security standard or a customer count you do not hold. Do not use a payment logo unless you are authorised to display it.

Copy you can adapt

Badge: Checkout, explained

Headline: No fine-print panic here

Body: Card details are entered on [PAYMENT PROVIDER]'s checkout page. Returns are accepted for [X] days. Support answers within [Y] on weekdays.

Links: Returns policy · Privacy · Talk to a human

Small print: Policies checked [DATE]. The full terms are on each linked page.

Replace every bracket with a fact you can prove. “Bank-level security” and “100% safe” are not facts; they are slogans that invite a complaint. Name the provider only if it is actually used, and follow its brand rules for any logo.

Set it up

  1. Open the Secure Checkout template in the studio.
  2. Replace the heading and body with one fact and one policy sentence.
  3. Point the three links at your real policy, privacy and contact pages.
  4. Set the trigger to Scroll depth at 85% and the frequency to once per day.
  5. Add URL rules for product and cart pages, and exclude checkout and payment paths.
  6. Save, publish and paste the install snippet using the installation guide.
  7. Test with a real policy question: can a visitor reach the answer in one tap from the panel?

What to measure

  • Clicks to the policy or support page. That is the panel doing its job.
  • Support questions the panel was meant to prevent. If the same question keeps arriving, the copy is not answering it.
  • Checkout completion before and after, over a comparable period.
  • Whether every claim is still true this month. A stale guarantee is worse than no guarantee.

PopupLoom does not attribute orders to the panel. Use your store's reporting and be careful about claiming causation from a single week of data.

Common mistakes

  • Using a vague badge such as “100% safe” with no evidence.
  • Implying a certification, audit or payment standard you do not hold.
  • Displaying a payment logo without permission or current brand approval.
  • Leaving an expired guarantee or holiday delivery promise live.
  • Covering the card fields on the payment step.
  • Linking to a policy page that does not exist or contradicts the panel.

Common questions

Does this popup make my store PCI compliant?

No. PCI compliance depends on your payment provider, your checkout setup and your own evidence. A popup is a layout for facts you supply; it cannot certify or change your compliance status.

Can I show payment card logos in the popup?

Only if you are authorised to use them and follow the brand's current guidelines. A logo implies that the card is accepted, not that the transaction is secure. Do not use a logo you have not earned or licensed.

Can I say the checkout is 100% secure?

No system is 100% secure, and an absolute claim is difficult to evidence. Describe the specific control you can prove, such as encrypted transmission through your payment provider, and link to the policy that explains it.

Where should the trust popup appear?

On product and cart pages before payment, not on the payment step itself. A panel that covers card fields while the visitor is entering them adds friction to the exact moment it was meant to reassure.
