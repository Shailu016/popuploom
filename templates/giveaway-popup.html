Free giveaway popup template

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 17 November 2026 Last updated 17 November 2026 Template checked 17 November 2026

A giveaway popup collects entries for a prize the site owner is actually awarding. The Bundle Giveaway template is a centred modal with an image slot for the prize, a countdown layout and a one-field entry form. It is the entry point to a promotion, not the promotion itself.

This page covers the rules to publish before the popup goes live, the difference between a countdown and a deadline, what the form stores, and the parts of a giveaway that a popup cannot run. It is one of the gamified templates, alongside the spin-to-win wheel.

PopupLoom does not run the draw. It stores entries and shows the copy you write. It does not verify eligibility, pick a random winner, detect duplicate accounts or deliver the prize. Those steps need a documented process and, often, a second tool.

What this template is for

The modal is deliberately simple: one prize, one email field, one button. A giveaway works when the prize is relevant to the audience and the rules are easy to find. It fails when the prize is a trick to collect addresses.

  • A product bundle from your own stock, with a stated value.
  • A launch prize tied to a new collection or feature.
  • An event draw with a fixed entry window.
  • A newsletter incentive where the prize and the list are related.

It is not a way to make a weak offer interesting. If nobody wants the product, nobody wants the prize built from it.

Publish the rules before the popup

A giveaway without rules is a dispute waiting for a winner. Put the rules on a stable page and link them from the modal, even if the link is small.

  • Sponsor: the legal name behind the promotion.
  • Entry window: start and end date, including timezone.
  • Eligibility: age, country or region, and any exclusions.
  • How to enter: the form, and whether a purchase is necessary.
  • Prize: what it is, how many, and its approximate value.
  • Odds: dependent on entries; do not imply fixed odds if they are not fixed.
  • Draw and notification: how and when the winner is chosen and contacted.
  • Data use: what happens to an entrant's address, and how to opt out.

If your rules say no purchase is necessary, do not require one. If the prize is only available in one country, say so before the form rather than after the entry.

Use it when

  • You have the prize in hand and can deliver it.
  • The rules are written and reviewed for the audiences you target.
  • You have a process for verifying entries and contacting a winner.
  • You can remove the popup when the entry window closes.

Do not run a giveaway to “grow the list” with no plan to contact the winner, and do not use a countdown that resets on reload. Both turn a promotion into a credibility problem.

Copy you can adapt

Badge: Win the bundle

Headline: One winner. Everything in the bundle.

Body: Enter your email to register one entry. The draw closes [DATE] at [TIME] [TIMEZONE].

Button: Enter the draw

Small print: [AGE]+ and [REGION] only. No purchase necessary. One entry per person. Rules and prize details.

Replace every bracket. “One entry per person” is only true if you can enforce it; if you cannot, write what you can actually verify. The copy generator can vary the structure, but it cannot write the rules for you.

Set it up

  1. Open the Bundle Giveaway template in the studio.
  2. Write the prize, entry window and eligibility into the copy and the linked rules page.
  3. Keep one required email field. Add a name field only if the winner needs to be addressed.
  4. Set the trigger to Scroll depth at 60% and the frequency to once per visitor.
  5. Set the countdown to the real end date, including the timezone in the copy.
  6. Save, publish and paste the install snippet using the installation guide.
  7. Submit a test entry and confirm it appears in the lead inbox with the expected fields.
  8. Put a calendar reminder to remove the popup when entries close.

After the entry window

Remove the popup or replace it with a “entries are closed” message. Export the entries as CSV from the lead inbox, then run the draw in a way you can describe: a random-number tool with a recorded seed, a manual draw witnessed by a colleague, or the promotion platform you used. Keep the record with the rules.

Contact the winner using the address they entered, ask for any delivery details you need, and do not publish their name or photo without permission. If the prize is not claimed within the stated period, follow the rules you published rather than inventing a new process.

What to measure

  • Entries and the cost of the prize per entry.
  • Duplicate and invalid addresses, which show whether verification is needed.
  • Winner response and delivery time. An unclaimed prize is a failed promotion.
  • Complaints and rule questions. These usually point to copy that was too vague.
  • List quality after the promotion, including unsubscribes and bounces.

PopupLoom reports stored responses and a de-duplicated view count. It does not report entry quality, fraud or the source of an entrant.

Common mistakes

  • Running a giveaway without published rules.
  • Implying odds or a draw the product does not enforce.
  • Requiring a purchase when the rules say no purchase is necessary.
  • Collecting entries with no plan to contact or fulfill the winner.
  • Leaving the modal live after the entry window.
  • Assuming the popup verifies age, region or duplicate entries.

Common questions

Does PopupLoom pick the winner?

No. The template collects entries. The draw, winner verification, notification and prize delivery are the owner's responsibility and should follow published rules.

Can PopupLoom stop duplicate or bot entries?

No. It stores the responses it receives and does not detect duplicate addresses, automated submissions or fraud. If the prize is valuable, verify entries in the system that runs the promotion.

Does the countdown end the giveaway automatically?

No. The countdown is a display layout with the target date you set. The popup keeps accepting entries until you publish a new version or turn it off, so remove it when the entry window closes.

Is a giveaway popup legal cover?

No. Promotion rules, eligibility, registration and tax obligations vary by country and sometimes by state. The popup is a layout; have the promotion reviewed where the prize or audience requires it.
