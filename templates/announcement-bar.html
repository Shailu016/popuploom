Free announcement bar template

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 16 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 Template checked 16 November 2026

An announcement bar is a single line of text at the top of the page. It carries one message that applies to the whole visit: a shipping threshold, a launch date, a service notice or a store closure. The template is built for scanning, not persuasion — a short label, one sentence and one link.

Unlike a modal, the bar does not ask for anything. That makes it the safest popup format on the site and the easiest to overuse. This page covers placement, the frequency rule, the copy to adapt and the layout mistakes that make bars feel like ads.

What this template is for

  • One message. Free shipping over a threshold, a holiday closure, a new location, a maintenance window.
  • One link. The bar should lead somewhere useful: the cart, the offer page, the delivery policy.
  • A dismiss control. A close button that is visible on both desktop and phone.
  • No form. If you need an email address, use the email signup template instead.

The bar is also the one format where a link to a policy is often the point. A shipping promise that is not linked to the shipping page is a claim without a source.

Use it when

  • The message changes a decision for most visitors, not a few.
  • The information is true today and will still be true when someone revisits.
  • You can keep it to one line on a 390px-wide screen.
  • It is not a substitute for the page's own headline. A bar that repeats the hero is noise.

Skip the bar for a single product launch, a blog post or a page where the navigation is already tight. On small screens, a bar plus a sticky header can consume a third of the viewport before the content starts.

Copy you can adapt

Label: SHIPPING

Message: Free delivery over [AMOUNT]. Orders below that are [AMOUNT].

Link text: Delivery details

Close label: Dismiss

The message should survive being read alone, without the page around it. If it needs a paragraph to explain, it belongs on a page, not in a bar.

Set it up

  1. Open the Marquee Masthead template in the studio.
  2. Keep the message to one line at 390px. Shorten the wording rather than shrinking the font.
  3. Set the trigger to page load and the placement to the top bar.
  4. Exclude checkout, terms and privacy from the URL rule.
  5. Set the frequency to once per session and confirm the close control is keyboard reachable.
  6. Save, publish and paste the two-line snippet. The installation guide covers placement and caching.

What to measure

  • Click-through on the bar's link, if the destination matters.
  • Dismissal timing. Instant dismissal suggests the bar is covering something or repeating the page.
  • Mobile layout. Check that the bar does not overlap the header or the first heading.
  • Layout stability. Watch for content jumping when the bar loads.

Common mistakes

  • Rotating three messages in a strip of text, so none of them is read.
  • Putting a countdown in the bar and resetting it on reload.
  • Letting the bar cover the navigation or the first line of the page on a phone.
  • Linking to a policy page that does not contain the promise made in the bar.
  • Showing the bar on checkout, where it competes with the order summary.

Common questions

Should an announcement bar be sticky or static?

A sticky bar stays visible while scrolling and is best for a shipping threshold or a deadline that affects the whole visit. A static bar is part of the page flow and cannot cover content, but it disappears as soon as the visitor scrolls. Pick one and check that it does not hide the navigation.

Does an announcement bar hurt page speed?

The bar itself is small, but anything that changes layout after load can shift content and hurt perceived speed. A fixed-position bar avoids pushing the page down. Test with your own performance tooling rather than assuming.

How many messages should rotate in the bar?

One. Rotation is a carousel in a strip of text: most visitors read the first message, and a moving bar competes with the page. If you have three things to say, pick the one that changes behaviour and put the rest elsewhere.

Can the bar be dismissed permanently?

The frequency rule decides that. A once-per-session setting brings the bar back on the next visit; a cookie-based dismissal keeps it hidden longer. Check how the rule behaves on your own site before promising visitors it will stay closed.
