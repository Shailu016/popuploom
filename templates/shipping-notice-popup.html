Free shipping notice popup template

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 17 November 2026 Last updated 17 November 2026 Template checked 17 November 2026

A shipping notice bar tells every visitor the same operational fact at the top of the page. The Shipping Delay template is a full-width top bar with one sentence and a link to the details. It is for a delay, a cutoff date or a temporary change to how orders are handled — not for a discount.

This page covers when a notice bar helps, how to word a delay without guessing, where the bar collides with fixed headers, and the removal step most stores forget. It is one of the announcement templates, which also includes launch, event and holiday-hours layouts.

A shipping notice is an operational commitment. If the bar says orders ship Monday, the store has to ship Monday or update the bar. Do not use a countdown or a vague “soon” to avoid naming the date.

What this template is for

The bar answers a question the visitor would otherwise ask support. It is most useful when the answer applies to everyone and is likely to change within days or weeks.

  • A courier delay with a revised dispatch date.
  • A holiday cutoff for delivery before a specific date.
  • A temporary closure for stocktaking, relocation or a public holiday.
  • A service change such as a new carrier or a region that is temporarily excluded.

It is not a marketing slot. A discount, a launch or a newsletter ask belongs in a different template, and mixing the two makes the operational message look like an ad.

Write the fact, the date and the link

A useful notice has three parts: what changed, when it applies, and where the full details live. Anything else is noise on a bar that follows the visitor across the site.

  • What: “Orders placed this week ship Monday.”
  • When: “This applies to orders placed 17–21 November.”
  • Where: “Read the delivery notice” links to a page with the carrier update and support hours.

If you do not know the new date, say what you know and when you will update: “We are confirming dispatch dates and will update this notice on 18 November.” That is more honest than a promise the warehouse cannot keep.

Use it when

  • The fact affects most visitors, not a single order.
  • You can link to a page with the full details and a contact route.
  • Someone owns the notice and will remove it when the situation changes.
  • The bar can be read in one line on a phone.

Do not use it for a one-off customer issue, a vague apology or a sale. Do not leave it up after normal service resumes; a stale delay notice trains visitors to ignore every notice.

Copy you can adapt

Notice: Orders placed 17–21 November ship Monday 23 November.

Supporting line: Tracking still updates normally. Our courier is catching up after the storm.

Button: Read the delivery notice

Link target: /shipping.html#delay (a real page with the carrier update and support hours)

Replace the dates and the link. If the carrier has not given you a date, do not invent one. The copy swipe file has shorter notice patterns if the first line will not fit on a phone.

Set it up

  1. Open the Shipping Delay template in the studio.
  2. Replace the sentence with the current fact and the link with a real details page.
  3. Set the trigger to Page load and the frequency to once per day.
  4. Exclude checkout, account and legal pages in the URL rules.
  5. Save, publish and paste the install snippet using the installation guide.
  6. Test on a phone: the bar must not cover the navigation or the first line of content. Add top spacing to a fixed header if it does.
  7. Put a calendar reminder to remove or update the bar on the date named in the notice.

Mobile, accessibility and the fixed header

A top bar takes vertical space from every page. On a phone, that space is scarce: a two-line notice can push the product title below the fold. Keep the sentence to one line at 360 pixels, or shorten the mobile copy rather than shrinking the font.

Check contrast between the text and the bar, and make the close control large enough to tap. If the bar cannot be dismissed, it must not cover navigation or block the first action on the page. A notice that traps the visitor is worse than the delay it describes.

What to measure

  • Clicks to the details page. That is the bar doing its job beyond the headline.
  • Support questions about the same delay. If they continue, the notice is not specific enough.
  • Mobile overlap and bounce on the first screen. Check after publishing, not only in the studio preview.
  • Whether the bar was removed on time. A stale notice is the most common failure.

PopupLoom counts a de-duplicated view and any link or form action it can record. It does not show dismissal rate or a per-page breakdown, so use your own analytics if you need those numbers.

Common mistakes

  • Covering navigation or the first line of content.
  • Leaving a stale notice after the delay is over.
  • Writing a vague notice with no date or link.
  • Using the bar for a discount and diluting the operational message.
  • Stacking a delay bar and a free-shipping bar on the same page.
  • Naming a dispatch date the warehouse has not confirmed.

Common questions

Will the notice cover my site header?

A top bar is fixed to the top of the viewport. If your theme also has a fixed header, the bar can overlap it. Add top spacing to the header or use a static placement, and test at phone widths before publishing.

Can I schedule the notice to start and stop automatically?

The template does not include a start and end scheduler. It appears under the trigger and frequency you set. Publish it when the notice is true and turn it off or replace it when the situation changes.

Can I show it only on product pages?

Yes. Use the popup's URL rules to include the paths that need the notice and exclude checkout, account and legal pages. A shipping delay does not need to follow a visitor into the payment step.

Is this the same as a free-shipping threshold bar?

No. This bar carries an operational notice such as a delay or a cutoff date. A free-shipping bar advertises a spend threshold. Use one or the other, not both at the same time.
