Free shipping bar template for ecommerce

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 16 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 Template checked 16 November 2026

A free shipping bar states the delivery threshold at the top of the store. It is a single line: spend this much and delivery is free, with a link to the full policy. The template is built for clarity, not persuasion, because the one thing it must never do is claim to know what is in the visitor's cart.

That is the key limitation: PopupLoom cannot read cart totals. The bar can state the threshold, but it cannot say “you are $12 away” or update as items are added. Copy that pretends otherwise breaks the moment the number is wrong, and a shipping promise that contradicts checkout is worse than no bar at all.

What this template is for

  • One threshold. The real amount, in the store's currency, that triggers free delivery.
  • One link. The delivery page, so the promise has a source.
  • Static copy. No progress bar, no remaining-amount counter, no invented countdown.
  • A dismiss control. Visible on desktop and phone, reachable by keyboard.

Use it when

  • Free delivery is a real offer with published terms.
  • The threshold is stable enough that the bar will not be wrong next week.
  • The message fits one line at 390px.
  • You are not already running a second announcement strip on the same pages.

Skip it during a sitewide sale if the sale already includes free delivery. Two messages about shipping in one viewport make the offer look conditional, even when it is not.

Copy you can adapt

Label: DELIVERY

Message: Free standard delivery on orders over [AMOUNT].

Link text: Delivery times and costs

Close label: Dismiss

If delivery is free only in some regions, say so in the bar or put the region in the link text. “Free delivery” followed by a checkout that charges for the visitor's country is the fastest way to lose the order.

Set it up

  1. Open the Free Shipping Rail template in the studio.
  2. Replace the amount and currency. Keep the sentence short enough for one mobile line.
  3. Point the link at the delivery policy page, not the homepage.
  4. Set the placement to the top bar and the trigger to page load.
  5. Exclude checkout, terms and privacy from the URL rule.
  6. Set the frequency to once per session and publish the two-line snippet.
  7. Check a phone viewport: the bar must not cover the navigation or the first heading.

What to measure

  • Clicks to the delivery page, which show whether the message raises questions.
  • Support questions about shipping, especially after launch.
  • Cart value distribution, to see whether the threshold sits near a realistic basket size.
  • Layout stability on mobile, since a fixed bar is less disruptive than one that pushes content.

Common mistakes

  • Copy that implies live progress, such as “you are almost there”, when the bar cannot read the cart.
  • A threshold that differs from the one applied at checkout.
  • Stacking the shipping bar with a second announcement bar.
  • Covering the navigation on small screens.
  • Leaving the bar on checkout, where it competes with the order summary.

Need cart-aware messaging? That requires reading cart state, which a script-based popup cannot do by itself. Either add the logic in your store theme and trigger the popup with a click, or use a store app that exposes the cart total. The nine-check buyer's checklist covers how to evaluate that requirement.

Common questions

Does the bar update as the cart total changes?

No. PopupLoom does not read cart totals, so the bar cannot show how much the visitor has left to spend. Use static copy that states the threshold, and link to the delivery page for the full terms.

Where should the shipping bar appear?

At the top of shopping pages, including the cart, and not on checkout, terms or privacy. Checkout already shows the shipping cost and a second promise there can contradict the order summary.

Should the bar be dismissible?

Yes. A threshold message is useful once and annoying on every page. A visible close control and a once-per-session frequency keep it from sitting on top of the navigation.

Can I stack the shipping bar with an announcement bar?

Technically yes, visually a bad idea. Two strips plus a sticky header can consume a large part of a phone screen before the content starts. Pick the message that changes behaviour and retire the other.
