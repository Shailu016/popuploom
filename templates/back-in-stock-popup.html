A back-in-stock popup captures an email from someone who wants a product that is currently unavailable. It opens when the visitor presses a notify-me button, asks for one address and confirms what happens next. The value is intent: the visitor has already chosen the product, so the popup only has to remove uncertainty.

The important limitation is on this page, not buried in a footnote: PopupLoom does not watch inventory and does not send the notification email. It captures the address, stores it in the lead inbox and hands it to you as CSV or a webhook. The rest of the sequence is yours.

What this template is for

  • The out-of-stock product page. One message: this item is unavailable, we can tell you when it returns.
  • One field. Email is enough. A phone number or size selector adds friction before the product exists again.
  • A clear promise. Say when the email will arrive, or say that it depends on the restock. Do not imply a date you do not control.
  • A visible close. Some visitors only want to read the restock date, not join a list.

Use it when

  • The product is genuinely unavailable, not merely low on stock.
  • You can send the follow-up yourself, or connect the webhook to a system that can.
  • You can identify which product the signup belongs to, either from the page or from a field.
  • You have a consent line that matches how you will use the address.

Do not show it on in-stock products or on collection pages where nothing is unavailable. A notify-me prompt on a product someone can buy today is confusing and collects low-intent addresses.

Copy you can adapt

Badge: OUT OF STOCK

Headline: We can tell you when it is back

Subheadline: Leave your email and we will write once when [PRODUCT] returns. No date promised.

Button: Notify me

Small print: One email about this product. Unsubscribe any time.

If you cannot promise a single email, change the small print. The promise in the popup is the one a visitor will hold you to.

Set it up

  1. Open the Back in Stock template in the studio.
  2. Put the product context in the copy, or add a field the visitor fills, so the signup is attributable.
  3. Set the trigger to Click and enter the selector for your notify button.
  4. Add a URL rule for product paths and exclude checkout.
  5. Publish, then paste the two-line snippet. Confirm the widget loads on the product page.
  6. Press the notify button on the published page and submit a test address.
  7. Export the test row, or confirm the webhook delivery, before announcing the feature.

The collect and export leads guide covers the inbox, CSV and signed webhook in detail.

What to measure

  • Signups per unavailable product, not one sitewide number.
  • Fulfilment time: how long between restock and the email you send.
  • List decay: addresses collected months ago are less likely to convert when the item returns.
  • Unsubscribe rate against the promise you made.

Common mistakes

  • Promising an automatic email that nothing in the stack will send.
  • Showing the popup on in-stock products because the URL rule is too broad.
  • Collecting addresses without recording which product they refer to.
  • Sending a marketing newsletter to a list that was promised a single restock notice.
  • Leaving the notify button visible when the product returns.

Common questions

Does PopupLoom send the back-in-stock email automatically?

No. It captures the address into the lead inbox, exports it as CSV or forwards it to a webhook. Sending the notification is your job, through your email provider or store app.

Can the popup detect whether a product is in stock?

Not natively. PopupLoom does not read inventory or product state. The practical approach is a click trigger on your own notify button, shown only on out-of-stock pages, or a URL rule that targets those pages.

How do I know which product a signup is for?

The simplest reliable method is one popup per product page group with the product name in the popup copy, or a form field the visitor fills. Do not assume the captured email alone tells you the SKU.

Should the popup open on page load or on a click?

On a click. The visitor has already shown intent by pressing notify me; opening a modal on load interrupts people who are only checking the price. A click trigger also keeps the list cleaner.
