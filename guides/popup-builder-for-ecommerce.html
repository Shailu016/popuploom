Free popup builder for ecommerce stores

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom · Last updated 16 November 2026

Reviewed 6 November 2026. This guide describes what the PopupLoom product can and cannot do for an online store. It was not tested on a live Shopify, WooCommerce or BigCommerce checkout; verify platform limits on your own store before publishing.

An ecommerce storefront has four useful popup moments: a first-time visitor deciding whether to browse, a shopper about to leave a product page, a cart with an unanswered objection, and a buyer who has already ordered. PopupLoom gives you a visual editor, 53 starting templates and a hosted install snippet for those moments — without a monthly bill.

  • No per-view feature meter. All triggers, targeting rules, device settings and content blocks are available on every popup.

Six ecommerce popups worth building first

Start with one popup per job. Stacking several modals on the same page usually costs more attention than it earns.

1. Welcome offer or first-order code

Use First-Order Code or Don't Leave Empty on the homepage or a campaign landing page. Keep the offer specific, state the real minimum spend and expiry, and show it once per visitor. If you collect an email, say what will be sent and link your privacy policy in the fine print.

PopupLoom does not generate unique single-use codes. Create the code in your store platform and paste the same code into the popup. That means a shared code can leak to coupon sites; use a short expiry or a store-side rule if that matters to your margin.

2. Cart rescue on exit intent

Cart Rescue, Comeback Code and Cancel Saver are built for cart and account pages. Target paths such as /cart* or /checkout* and exclude order-confirmation pages.

Be precise about what the product knows: PopupLoom cannot read cart contents, cart value or customer identity. The popup is a message and a form, not a cart integration. Do not write copy that pretends to know the shopper's items unless your own page renders that context.

Exit intent is desktop only. On phones, use a delay, scroll depth or a click trigger instead, and keep the popup out of the checkout step.

3. Free-shipping threshold bar

Free Shipping Rail and Marquee Masthead are thin announcement bars rather than modals. They suit a real free-shipping threshold, a delivery cut-off or a store-wide notice. A bar interrupts less than a full-screen overlay and can stay visible while the shopper adds items.

State the threshold and currency exactly. If free shipping excludes remote postcodes, oversized items or sale stock, say so in the linked terms instead of hiding it in fine print.

4. Back-in-stock and low-stock notices

Back in Stock and Low-Stock Batch work on product pages. Back-in-stock capture is a form: the visitor leaves an email or phone number and you follow up when the item returns. PopupLoom stores the response; it does not watch inventory or send the alert for you.

Only publish low-stock or countdown copy when the number is real and current. Fake scarcity is both a trust problem and, in many markets, a consumer-protection problem.

5. Bundle and price-watch prompts

Bundle Builder and Price Watch are informational popups for product or collection pages. Use them to explain a real bundle rule or to collect interest in a price drop. They do not calculate a bundle discount or track prices automatically.

6. Post-purchase review and loyalty

Review Request and Loyalty Card belong after the order, not before it. Target a thank-you page path if your platform exposes one. On Shopify, theme-code popups do not automatically carry into checkout or the order-status page; treat those surfaces as separate and test them.

Choose the trigger by page, not by habit

  • Homepage: a delay of 20–40 seconds or desktop exit intent, once per week. A page-load modal on the homepage is the most common way to lose a first-time visitor.
  • Collection page: scroll depth around 50–70%, so the popup appears after the shopper has seen products rather than before.
  • Product page: desktop exit intent, or a delay on mobile, once per day. Match the offer to the product, not to a generic newsletter.
  • Cart page: exit intent or a click on a help link. This is a high-intent moment; keep the copy short and the close button obvious.
  • Content and blog: a lead magnet at scroll depth, not a discount. A shopper reading a buying guide may not be ready for a coupon.
  • Thank-you page: a review request or referral, once ever, and only if the page is not inside a restricted checkout flow.

Frequency rules are part of the product: every load, once per day, once per week, once ever, or keep showing until dismissed and then rest for a day. Use the strictest rule that still lets the campaign work.

Make the offer fit the margin

A popup can present an offer, but it cannot make the economics work. Before publishing a discount, calculate the gross margin after the discount, payment fees, shipping and returns. A 20% code on a 25% margin store can turn a sale into a loss. A free-shipping threshold is often safer than a sitewide percentage because it raises average order value instead of cutting every order.

Also decide how the offer is limited: first order only, minimum spend, one use per customer, excluded categories, or a deadline. PopupLoom can display these terms, but enforcement happens in your store platform. Do not let the popup promise a restriction the checkout cannot enforce.

Respect the shopper

  • One popup per page. If two campaigns target the same URL, the visitor may see both.
  • Close must be obvious. Use a readable close control and keep it inside the visible viewport on small screens.
  • Do not trap focus or block the page. A popup should be dismissible with the keyboard as well as a pointer.
  • No fake countdowns or stock numbers. Countdown blocks are real timers; set a truthful target or leave the block out.
  • Consent before marketing. If you collect an email for a newsletter, the fine-print block should identify the sender and link a privacy policy. PopupLoom forms do not render a separate consent checkbox, so do not describe a checkbox the visitor cannot see.

Install on your storefront

The install is two lines: a widget element carrying your saved widget id and a script tag loading the PopupLoom runtime. Paste both before the closing </body> tag.

  • Shopify: duplicate the theme, edit layout/theme.liquid, publish and test the live domain. Checkout is separate.
  • WordPress and WooCommerce: a footer-code plugin, theme hook or Custom HTML block. Watch caching and script-combining plugins.
  • Webflow: site-wide custom code or a page-level embed, depending on your plan and publishing permissions.
  • Custom storefronts: add the snippet to the shared layout template. If your store is a single-page app, confirm that the runtime sees the route changes you want to target.

Platform limits are real. Shopify checkout, native mobile apps, marketplaces and iframe-only site builders can block or box the widget. Check the popup-not-showing guide if the snippet is present but nothing appears.

Measure what the popup actually did

Each saved popup shows views in My popups, and form submissions are stored in the lead inbox. Open and submit events are pushed to the Google Tag Manager data layer when GTM is present. PopupLoom does not attribute revenue or connect to your store's orders, so pair the popup numbers with your own analytics and checkout reports.

Compare one change at a time: trigger, offer, copy or design. A popup that gets more submissions but lower-quality leads is not automatically an improvement.

What PopupLoom does not do

  • It does not read cart contents, cart value or customer accounts.
  • It does not send abandoned-cart email, SMS or push notifications.
  • It does not generate unique coupon codes or validate them at checkout.
  • It does not provide A/B testing, revenue attribution or a native app SDK.
  • It does not inject into Shopify checkout, marketplace listings or native mobile apps.
  • It does not guarantee conversions. Results depend on traffic, offer, timing and audience.

If you need those capabilities, use your store platform and email or SMS provider alongside PopupLoom rather than expecting the popup layer to replace them.

Ecommerce popup questions

Is PopupLoom free for ecommerce stores?

Yes. Designing and previewing popups is free and needs no account. A free account is required to save a popup and get the hosted install code. There is no credit card, trial countdown or per-view billing option in the product.

Can I use PopupLoom on Shopify or WooCommerce?

You can paste the two-line embed into theme code on Shopify or into a WordPress footer or Custom HTML area for WooCommerce. Platform plans, script filters, caching and checkout rules vary, so test the published storefront. Popups installed in theme code do not automatically extend into Shopify checkout.

Can an ecommerce popup show the contents of a shopper's cart?

No. PopupLoom does not read cart contents or customer accounts. You can target cart and product URLs and write copy for that context, but the popup cannot display live cart items, cart value or customer names.

Does PopupLoom send abandoned-cart emails?

No. PopupLoom stores form responses in your lead inbox and can forward them to a webhook you configure. Sending email or SMS requires your own email, SMS or automation platform.

Can I create a first-order discount popup?

Yes. Build a welcome popup with the First-Order Code template, add your real terms and show it once per visitor or once per week. PopupLoom does not generate unique single-use coupon codes, so use your store platform to create and validate the code.

Do ecommerce popups work on mobile?

Yes. Popups render on phones, and device settings let you enable or disable desktop and phone separately. Exit intent is desktop only. Test the close button, text size and checkout flow on a real phone before publishing.
