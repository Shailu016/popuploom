An exit-intent popup appears when a desktop visitor moves the cursor out through the top of the page — usually toward the address bar, another tab or the back button. It is the last moment before a visit ends, which makes it a good place for a reminder, a first-order offer or a lead magnet. It is not a page-load popup, and it does not work on touch screens.

How exit intent actually works

The browser reports that the mouse left the document, with no element receiving it. PopupLoom listens for that event and checks that the pointer crossed the top edge of the viewport. If both are true, the popup fires once, subject to the frequency rule.

  • Desktop only. Touch devices have no cursor and no dependable "leaving" event. PopupLoom skips exit intent on mobile by design rather than guessing.
  • Top edge only. Moving the cursor to the side, to a second monitor or into devtools does not count.
  • Frequency still applies. Once per day, once per week or once ever can suppress a second appearance.
  • It is a trigger, not a design. The same popup can use a different trigger on mobile, such as a delay or scroll depth.

Add an exit-intent popup with PopupLoom

  1. Open the PopupLoom studio and pick a template, or start blank. A single offer works better than a menu of options.
  2. In the behavior settings, set the trigger to Exit intent. Choose a frequency that matches the offer: once per day for a discount, once ever for a welcome gift.
  3. Set page targeting. Exclude checkout, legal pages and anything the visitor needs to finish a task. A popup on a payment step is a conversion tax, not a conversion tool.
  4. Save the popup and copy the two-line install code from Publish.
  5. Paste it before </body> in your site footer, theme code, or a Custom HTML block. Platform steps: WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace and Wix.
  6. Publish the site and test the trigger on the live URL.

Test it correctly

  • Open a private window on a desktop with a mouse or trackpad.
  • Move the cursor quickly out through the top edge of the page. Do not switch tabs; that is a different event.
  • If nothing happens, check the frequency rule, page targeting, device setting and the browser console.
  • Submit one fake response and confirm it lands in the lead inbox.
  • Test the mobile trigger separately. Exit intent will not fire there.

Exit-intent best practices

  • One offer. A single discount, checklist or reminder. Multiple choices dilute the moment.
  • Do not fire on the first page load. Exit intent is strongest after the visitor has read something. Use page targeting or a short delay before the trigger is armed where your setup allows it.
  • Do not block the close button. PopupLoom renders a close control and supports Escape. Never hide it or make it tiny.
  • Respect frequency. Once per day is plenty for most stores. Once ever for a welcome offer.
  • Exclude checkout and account flows. A popup during payment is a policy and usability risk.
  • Have a mobile plan. Use a scroll or click trigger for phone visitors; do not pretend exit intent covers them.

What an exit-intent popup cannot do

It cannot read cart contents, cart value, customer accounts or order history. It cannot send the follow-up email, apply a discount code automatically or guarantee a sale. It captures the response and stores it; the offer, the code enforcement and the follow-up belong to you and your platform.

Exit-intent popup FAQ

What is an exit-intent popup?

A popup that appears when the cursor leaves the top of the viewport, usually as the visitor moves toward the address bar or back button.

Does it work on mobile?

No. Touch devices have no cursor and no reliable exit event. PopupLoom disables exit intent on mobile by design; use a timed, scroll or click trigger instead.

Is it free?

With PopupLoom, yes. There is no pageview cap or paid upgrade inside the product. A free account is required to save and install a widget.

Why is it not firing?

Check that the pointer exits through the top edge, that you are on desktop, that the frequency rule has not already recorded a view, and that the page is included in targeting.

Can I show it once per visitor?

Yes. Choose once per day, once per week or once ever. Frequency is stored in the visitor's browser.
