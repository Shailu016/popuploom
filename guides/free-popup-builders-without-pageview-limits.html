Popup pageview limits: how to audit a free plan in 20 minutes

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 14 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 Test plan checked 16 November 2026

“No pageview limits” is one of the most common phrases in popup marketing and one of the least precise. It can mean the account does not count pageviews. It can mean the free tier is generous enough that the vendor expects you not to notice. It can mean the limit moved to something else, such as one live campaign or one domain.

This guide is a test plan rather than a list. It takes about 20 minutes per product and works on any free plan, including ours. The aim is to leave with three numbers and one answer: the unit, your exposure, the price of the next tier, and what happens when you cross the line. If you are comparing free popup builders without pageview limits, this is how you verify the claim instead of trusting it.

Why not just rank the free plans? Because rankings expire. A plan that is generous in November can change its allowance in January, and a list that does not show you how to recheck it is a list you have to trust blindly. The audit below survives the next pricing update because it asks the vendor's page, not ours.

The four shapes of a “free” popup plan

  • No meter. The account does not charge by traffic at all. This is the strongest version of free, and the rarest. It still has rate limits and a feature list.
  • Recurring allowance. A monthly or daily allowance that resets. It can work indefinitely for a small site and binds abruptly for a growing one.
  • Trial. A one-time credit. It ends by design, sometimes by moving the account onto a paid plan automatically.
  • Feature-gated free. Generous traffic and a missing feature. The plan is free; the campaign you wanted is not possible.

Most confusion comes from mixing the second and fourth. A plan with 50,000 pageviews and one live popup is not generous for a store that needs three campaigns at once. A plan with one domain is not generous for an agency.

The 20-minute audit

Minutes 1–5: the plan card

Open the pricing page and write down four things: the unit, the number, the reset and the price of the tier above. Ignore the feature list for now. If the card says “5,000 pageviews per month”, you have the unit and the reset. If it says “5,000” without a unit, look for a tooltip or a footnote; if there is none, that is a finding in itself.

Minutes 5–10: the comparison table

Find the second and third limits. Look for rows labelled live campaigns, websites, domains, team members, contacts, credits or email quota. Note which features are marked only on paid tiers. Exit intent, scroll triggers, analytics and split testing are the usual gates.

Minutes 10–15: the trial install

Install the popup on one low-traffic page. Build one popup with one trigger. Submit a test response. The point is not to evaluate design quality; it is to confirm the install path works on your platform and that a response reaches the account.

Minutes 15–20: export and mobile check

Export the test row. Open the page in a private window on a phone and on a desktop. Turn caching on and repeat. If the popup disappears with caching active, or the export needs a support ticket, you have found the constraint that will matter later.

Record the four numbers in one place: unit, allowance, reset, next-tier annual price. You will reuse them when the plan changes.

Calculate your own exposure

The allowance only matters relative to your traffic. Use the formula that matches the vendor's unit:

Vendor metersYour numberWhat to compare
PageviewsMonthly pageviewsPageviews ÷ allowance = how many times over the cap you are.
Unique visitorsMonthly unique visitorsVisitors ÷ allowance. Do not use pageviews here.
ImpressionsVisitors × displays per visitorEstimated displays ÷ allowance.
Live campaignsCampaigns you need at onceCount required, not designs built.
DomainsSites you operateCount every brand, including staging if it runs the popup.

A simple example: 12,000 monthly pageviews against a 5,000-pageview allowance is 2.4× over, so the free tier binds after roughly 12 days of a 30-day month. The same site against a 10,000-visitor allowance may be comfortably inside it, because visitors and pageviews are different units. Five questions to send support

Pricing pages answer the easy questions. These are the ones that decide a migration:

  1. What happens when the allowance is reached: does the popup pause, does the account throttle, or is an overage billed?
  2. Does the allowance reset on a fixed date or on my billing anniversary, and does unused capacity carry over?
  3. Which features on the comparison table are unavailable on the free tier?
  4. Can I export submissions and contacts as CSV without a support ticket?
  5. If I cancel, how long are my historical responses retained?

Keep the answers. If a vendor will not put the at-limit behaviour in writing, treat the free tier as unpredictable and plan the campaign accordingly.

When a pageview limit actually matters

A cap is not automatically a problem. It matters when three conditions overlap:

  • Your traffic is close to the allowance, or will be within the campaign's lifetime.
  • The popup is permanent. A two-week launch can live inside a small allowance; a sitewide welcome popup cannot.
  • The next tier costs more than the popup is worth. If the paid plan is $40 a month and the popup generates two leads, the maths is not close.

If those do not overlap, a capped free plan is a sensible choice and there is no reason to switch. The no-limits page explains what our version of “free” does and does not cover, including the rate limits and the missing features.

Four findings that should stop a trial

Most audit findings are trade-offs you can live with. These four are different: each one means the plan cannot be trusted with a campaign.

  • The at-limit behaviour is undocumented. If nobody will tell you whether the popup pauses, throttles or drops responses, you cannot plan around the allowance.
  • The export does not work. If a test row cannot be exported during the free trial, assume it will not work at scale either.
  • The popup fails with caching active. A popup that only appears in preview is not installed. This is the most common false positive in a quick test.
  • The pricing page contradicts itself. Two different allowances or guarantee windows for the same plan mean the page is not maintained. Treat every number on it as provisional.

None of these is about design quality or feature count. They are about whether the plan behaves predictably under the conditions you will actually run it, which is the only thing a 20-minute audit can establish.

Common questions

What does “no pageview limit” mean?

It means the popup account does not count pageviews against an allowance. It does not mean unlimited hosting or unlimited features. Ask separately about rate limits, uptime commitments and which features are included.

How do I find a free plan's real limit?

Read the plan card for the unit and the reset, then read the feature table for a second limit such as one live campaign or one domain. The smaller of the two is usually what binds first.

What happens when a free popup plan hits its limit?

It depends on the vendor and is often not stated on the pricing page. Possible outcomes include pausing the popup, throttling the account, or billing an overage. Ask support and keep the written answer before you rely on the free tier.

Should I count pageviews or visitors?

Count the unit the vendor meters. If it meters pageviews, use your analytics pageview total; if it meters visitors, use unique visitors. Comparing a visitor number with a pageview allowance understates your exposure.

Is a free plan with a cap still worth using?

Yes, if your traffic stays comfortably below the cap and the missing features do not matter. A cap is only a problem when it is unpredictable or when the next tier costs more than the popup is worth.

How long does the audit take?

About 20 minutes per plan: five minutes on the pricing page, five on the plan comparison, five to install and submit a test response, and five to export and check the result on a phone.

Method and limitations

This is a vendor-neutral test plan. No competitor is named, no pricing is quoted and no account was created for this page. The formulas are arithmetic, and the example uses round numbers to make the unit difference visible.

Free plans change without notice. The audit tells you what a plan does today and what to ask before you depend on it; it cannot predict the next pricing update. No ranking, traffic or conversion claim is made here.
