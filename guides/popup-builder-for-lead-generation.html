Lead generation is a trade: something useful in exchange for a way to follow up. A popup is the handshake. Done well, it meets a visitor at a moment of intent with an offer that matches the page. Done badly, it interrupts the page before the visitor has decided whether the offer is worth anything. PopupLoom gives you the design, trigger and form for that handshake without a monthly bill.

Match the offer to the page

The most common lead-popup mistake is showing the same newsletter offer everywhere. A visitor reading a pricing page and a visitor reading a recipe are not in the same state of mind.

  • Blog article: a checklist, template, calculator or deeper guide at 50–70% scroll depth. Ask for an email only; do not demand a phone number.
  • Pricing page: a demo, a call or a comparison sheet at exit intent. This is a high-intent page, so a sales conversation is a fair exchange.
  • Webinar or event page: a seat reservation with a clear date and time. Use a click or delay trigger and show the timezone.
  • Newsletter or creator site: a weekly digest in a slim bottom bar, once per day. A full-screen modal on every article is usually too much.
  • Tool or calculator: save or email the result after the visitor has used the tool. The value is already proven.
  • Waitlist or launch page: a single email field and a clear statement of what happens next. One page, one action.
  • B2B service site: an audit, teardown or consultation on service and case-study pages. Exclude careers, support and legal pages.

The offer does not have to be a download. A quote, a callback, a checklist, a template, a recorded walkthrough or a place on a waitlist can all be a fair trade. What matters is that the popup states exactly what the visitor gets and what you will do with their details.

Seven lead-generation popup plays

1. Lead magnet shelf

Lead Magnet Shelf presents one or more resources and a short form. Use it on a resource hub or at the end of a long article. Name the format (PDF, Notion template, spreadsheet), the length and who it is for. “Free guide” without a topic is not an offer.

2. Webinar seat

Webinar Seat is a date-and-time offer. State the timezone, the duration, whether a recording is available and what happens after signup. PopupLoom stores the response but does not send a calendar invitation; your email or calendar tool has to do that.

3. Waitlist ticket

Queue Ticket suits a launch page. Keep it to an email field and one sentence about when the visitor will hear from you. If you cannot commit to a date, say “we will email when access opens” rather than inventing a countdown.

4. One small yes

One Small Yes is a low-friction newsletter or update request. It works where the visitor is not ready for a sales conversation: a glossary page, a comparison article or a changelog. Do not pretend a newsletter signup is a demo request.

5. Corner slide-in

Corner Peek is a small, persistent offer that does not cover the page. It is a good fit for a secondary lead magnet, a podcast signup or a “book a call” link on a long page. Because it is less intrusive, it can stay available longer than a modal.

6. Exit-intent save

Don't Leave Empty or a modal built from scratch can catch a desktop visitor who is leaving a pricing or demo page. Use copy that acknowledges the moment without being manipulative: “Before you go — want the comparison sheet?” works better than a fake warning.

Exit intent is desktop only in PopupLoom. On phones, use a delay, scroll depth or a click trigger. A mobile exit-intent clone that fires on every scroll reversal will feel broken.

7. Review or referral request

Review Request and Refer a Friend are not lead magnets in the strict sense, but they generate the same asset: a contact or a warm introduction. Use them after a purchase or a completed project, not on a first visit.

Choose triggers by intent

  • Exit intent (desktop): pricing, demo, comparison and checkout-adjacent pages. It is the last chance to offer something before the tab closes.
  • Scroll depth: articles and guides. Firing at 50–70% means the visitor has read enough to judge the offer.
  • Delay: landing pages and webinars. A short delay avoids covering the headline before it is read.
  • Click: a “get the template” or “save my results” button. This is the most explicit signal of intent and the least intrusive trigger.
  • Scroll to element: a specific section, such as a pricing table or a resource list.
  • Page load: only for a dedicated landing page where the popup is the page's purpose. Not on a homepage.

Frequency rules matter as much as the trigger. Once per week is a reasonable default for a newsletter; once ever for a launch or a high-value download; once per day for a persistent but optional slide-in.

Form design: ask only for what you will use

PopupLoom forms support text, email, phone, number, date and URL fields. Each field needs a name and a placeholder. Required fields are enforced before submission.

  • Start with email. Add a name only if you will personalise the follow-up.
  • Add one qualifier at most. Company size, role or budget can help routing, but every extra field lowers completion.
  • Use the fine-print block for expectations. Say what will be sent, how often and how to unsubscribe, and link your privacy policy.
  • Do not imply a consent checkbox. PopupLoom forms do not render a separate checkbox field. If your legal basis requires explicit checkbox consent, use a form tool that provides one or state the consent clearly in visible copy and keep a record in your follow-up system.
  • Keep the CTA specific. “Send me the checklist” is clearer than “Submit”.

Do not collect sensitive data through a popup. A name and a business email are usually enough; payment details, government identifiers and health information do not belong in a marketing form.

Where the lead actually goes

Responses are stored against the saved widget and are readable only by the account that owns it. The lead inbox pages results and includes a CSV export.

  • Browser webhook: posts the response as plain JSON from the visitor's browser. It is best effort, cannot be reliably confirmed or retried, and its URL is visible to visitors. Never put a private credential in it.
  • Server-side webhook: configured in the lead inbox, signs deliveries with HMAC-SHA256, retries transient failures and records a delivery log. The URL and secret are owner-only. Point it at your own endpoint or automation service.
  • CSV: a manual path for a CRM import or a spreadsheet. It is a snapshot, not a live sync.

There is no native HubSpot, Salesforce, Mailchimp or Klaviyo integration. If you need one, use the signed webhook and your own automation layer. PopupLoom does not send the follow-up email, SMS, PDF or calendar invite by itself.

Consent and privacy, without hand-waving

Rules differ by country and by the relationship you have with the visitor. PopupLoom does not provide legal compliance, and this guide is not legal advice. Practical basics:

  • Tell the visitor who is collecting the data and why.
  • Link a privacy policy that describes storage, processors, retention and contact rights.
  • Do not pre-tick or imply consent that the visitor did not give.
  • Give a real way to withdraw, and honour it in your follow-up system.
  • Collect the minimum needed for the offer.

Read the PopupLoom privacy and data handling page for what the product stores and how webhooks work.

Install and test the capture path

Paste the widget element and script before </body> on the pages where the offer should appear.

  • WordPress: footer code or a Custom HTML block; check caching and script optimisation.
  • Shopify: theme code covers the storefront, not checkout.
  • Webflow: site or page custom code, depending on your plan.
  • Custom sites: add the snippet to the shared layout and confirm the trigger on the published domain.

Test with fake data before you announce the campaign: submit the form, check the lead inbox, confirm the webhook delivery if you use one, and open the page in a private window to verify the frequency rule. The popup-not-showing guide covers the common failures.

Measure leads, not just views

Views and submissions are visible per popup. Open and submit events reach the Google Tag Manager data layer when GTM is present. PopupLoom does not calculate conversion rate or attribute revenue, so use your own analytics to compare visitors with submissions over the same period.

Watch lead quality as well as volume. A popup that collects many free-email addresses for a weak offer can look successful while producing nothing for sales. Track what happens after the handshake: reply rate, qualified rate and closed rate.

What PopupLoom does not do

  • It does not send email, SMS, push notifications, PDFs or calendar invitations.
  • It does not provide a CRM, marketing automation or native ESP integrations.
  • It does not score, enrich or verify leads.
  • It does not run A/B tests or calculate statistical significance.
  • It does not guarantee conversions, leads or revenue.

Those are jobs for the rest of your stack. The popup's job is to present the offer and capture the response reliably.

Lead-generation popup questions

Is PopupLoom free for lead generation?

Yes. You can design and preview lead capture popups without an account. A free account is required to save a widget, install it and collect responses in the lead inbox. There is no credit card or trial timer in the product.

What is a lead magnet popup?

A lead magnet popup offers something useful, such as a checklist, template, guide or webinar seat, in exchange for contact details. The popup is the form and the offer; delivering the file or email sequence is handled by your own tools.

Does PopupLoom send the lead magnet automatically?

No. PopupLoom stores the form response and can forward it to a webhook. Sending the PDF, email sequence, SMS or calendar invitation requires your own email, CRM or automation platform.

Can I export captured leads to CSV?

Yes. The lead inbox includes a CSV export for the responses owned by your account. Access is authenticated, so only the widget owner can read the stored leads.

Can I connect a popup to my CRM?

There is no native CRM integration. You can use a browser webhook, which is best effort and visible to visitors, or an authenticated server-side webhook that signs deliveries with HMAC-SHA256, retries transient failures and records a delivery log. Point that webhook at your own endpoint or automation service.

Should I use exit intent for lead generation?

Exit intent can work well on pricing, demo and high-intent content pages, but it is desktop only in PopupLoom. Use scroll depth, a delay or a click trigger for phone visitors, and test one change at a time.
