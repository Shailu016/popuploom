Best free popup builders in 2026: an honest comparison

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 14 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 All six vendors checked 16 November 2026

There is no single best free popup builder. The right answer depends on your platform and on the unit the free plan meters. Popup Maker is the strongest free choice on WordPress. PopupLoom is the strongest free choice for a non-WordPress site that can add a script. OptiMonk has the largest free pageview allowance. Poptin bundles email and coupons behind a 1,000-visitor cap. Popupsmart and OptinMonster are better understood as paid products with a trial-sized free entry.

This page is a documentation review, not a product test. We read each vendor's own pricing and product pages on 16 November 2026. No accounts were created, no campaigns were run, and no conversion benchmark is claimed.

Most “best free popup builder” lists are affiliate lists. They rank by commission, repeat the vendor's headline number and skip the unit. That is how a 1,000-visitor cap and a 10,000-pageview cap end up looking equivalent, when one is four times stricter on a site with long sessions.

This page does the opposite. It names the unit, the cap, the platform and the paid cliff for each tool, then says who each one fits. PopupLoom is our product, so its weaknesses are listed alongside the others. If the honest answer for your site is a competitor, that answer is in the table.

How we compared them

Five criteria, all checkable on the vendor's own pages:

  • The unit. Pageviews, unique visitors, campaign impressions or no meter at all. The unit decides how fast a free tier runs out.
  • The cap and its reset. A monthly allowance that resets is different from a one-time trial. OptinMonster's 500 impressions do not come back.
  • Platform and install. WordPress plugin, script snippet or platform app. A tool that cannot run on your site is not cheap at any price.
  • What the free tier removes. Branding, live-campaign limits, feature gates and the price of the first paid plan.
  • Data and exit. Where leads are stored and whether you can export them without a support ticket.

We did not score the editors, measure load time or run conversion tests. Those need hands-on work on the same site, and a single test would not generalise to yours.

The six tools, reviewed by fit

1. Popup Maker — best free option on WordPress

Free, uncapped, self-hosted, WordPress-only. Version 1.25.0, more than 600,000 active installs and a 4.9/5 rating from 4,516 reviews on WordPress.org.

The free plugin is a real popup builder, not a demo. It creates popups, slide-ins and announcement bars with auto-open, click and form-submission triggers, page and role conditions, and no traffic cap because the software runs on your server. Submissions live in your WordPress database, so you own the data and the backups.

The catch is the trigger list. Exit intent, scroll triggers, popup analytics, advanced targeting, scheduling and split testing are Pro extensions inside a $99-a-year bundle, discounted to $59.40 for the first year at the time of checking. A WordPress site that wants exit intent is comparing Pro with the alternatives, not the free plugin.

Choose it if you run WordPress and want native, self-hosted popups with no vendor script and no meter. Skip it if you are not on WordPress, or you need exit intent and do not want another subscription. Full detail: PopupLoom vs Popup Maker.

2. PopupLoom — best free option off WordPress

Free, no meter, no paid tier, any site that allows a script. 45 templates, six triggers, a lead inbox, CSV export and a signed webhook.

PopupLoom is a hosted widget. Two lines go before the closing body tag, the configuration is fetched from PopupLoom, and edits go live without re-pasting the snippet. There is no pageview, visitor or impression meter, which is the reason it appears first on the no-limits comparison as well as here.

The feature set is deliberately small. No exit-intent or scroll trigger, no A/B testing, no analytics dashboard, no AI, no email sending, no cart or coupon logic and no WordPress admin integration. Leads are stored in the lead inbox and moved out by CSV or webhook; the configuration is hosted, so it is not as local as a plugin.

Choose it if you are on Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix or a custom build, want one or two focused popups and do not want a traffic meter. Skip it if you are on WordPress, need exit intent, testing or analytics, or want the data to stay entirely on your own server.

3. OptiMonk — largest free pageview allowance

10,000 pageviews, 1,000 AI credits, 1 domain, unlimited campaigns, no branding. Paid plans from $228 a year.

On paper this is the most generous free tier in the group: twice Popupsmart's pageviews and ten times Poptin's visitors, with no campaign limit. For a small store that wants a welcome popup, an exit-intent popup and a quiz running at once, it is a credible zero-cost starting point.

Two things temper it. The plan also meters AI credits, and the pricing page does not publish the credit cost of an AI action, so the real free ceiling is unclear if you use the AI. And the next tier jumps from 20,000 to 100,000 pageviews, which makes the middle of the range expensive per thousand. Premium adds a key account manager at $2,148 a year.

Choose it if you want the most free traffic headroom, AI-assisted campaigns and a platform that can grow into quizzes and upsells. Skip it if you need more than one domain for free or you will not use the AI. Full detail: PopupLoom vs OptiMonk.

4. Poptin — popups, coupons and email in one place

1,000 unique visitors a month, one domain, unlimited popups and forms, API access. Paid plans from $240 a year.

Poptin's free plan is unusual for what it includes rather than how much traffic it allows. Unlimited popups and forms, API access, 60+ templates and support are all on the free tier; the visible exclusions are the branding credit, client subaccounts and team management. If your popup is the front door to an email programme or a coupon campaign, having those in the same account is a real advantage.

The meter is the constraint. One thousand unique visitors a month is a small site or a single landing page. Basic raises it to 10,000 for $240 a year, Pro to 50,000 for $564 and Agency to 150,000 for $1,140, all billed annually in the default view.

Choose it if you want popups, coupons and email contacts in one dashboard and your free traffic is genuinely small. Skip it if you already have email and only need the popup, because the cap becomes a subscription for capacity. Full detail: PopupLoom vs Poptin.

5. Popupsmart — most designs, but one live campaign

5,000 pageviews, one website, one live popup campaign, 100 email quota. Paid plans from $390 a year.

Popupsmart lists more than 500 conversion-optimised designs and a deep targeting list: cookies, geolocation, traffic source, new versus returning visitors, HTML content and more. Advanced and above add A/B split testing and campaign analytics. For an ecommerce team that wants cart, coupon and recommendation popups, the campaign types are there.

The free tier is more limited than the pageview number suggests. You can create unlimited popups but only one can be live, so a welcome popup and a cart-rescue popup cannot run together. Paid plans start at $390 for 50,000 pageviews and three websites. The pricing page also prints two different money-back windows in two places; pin down which applies before buying.

Choose it if you need many designs and run several campaigns across several sites on a paid plan. Skip it if you need more than one live popup for free or your pageviews are close to 5,000. Full detail: PopupLoom vs Popupsmart.

6. OptinMonster — a trial, not a free plan

500 campaign impressions, then auto-upgrade to Plus. Paid plans from a promotional $108 first year on Basic; Plus is $570 a year list.

OptinMonster is a mature platform with more than 700 templates, exit-intent campaigns, A/B testing from Plus, geo and retargeting from Growth and a WordPress plugin with over a million active installs. It is operated by Retyp, LLC. For a marketer running a campaign calendar, the product is capable.

It does not belong in a free-builder list on equal terms. Five hundred impressions is a short trial, and it ends by moving the account onto a paid plan rather than pausing. Basic is the cheapest tier but exit intent sits at Pro, so the real entry price for the feature most people want is higher than the headline.

Choose it if you want the template library and testing depth and you are ready to pay after a trial. Skip it if you need a free tier that keeps working next month. Full detail: PopupLoom vs OptinMonster.

How to choose in five questions

  1. What platform are you on? WordPress: start with Popup Maker. Everything else: PopupLoom is the only no-meter free option in this group.
  2. What does the free plan count? Convert your traffic into the vendor's unit before comparing numbers. Pageviews and visitors are not interchangeable.
  3. How many campaigns must run at once? Popupsmart's free tier allows one. If you need three, opt out of that tier immediately.
  4. Which trigger do you actually need? A page-load welcome popup is free almost everywhere. Exit intent is a paid feature on Popup Maker and OptinMonster, and absent from PopupLoom.
  5. Where must the data live? Plugin: your database. Hosted: the vendor's, with export. Decide before you collect the first lead.

What “free” means in each plan

The word hides four different things:

  • No meter. PopupLoom and Popup Maker. Traffic does not change the price. This is the only version of “free” that stays free as you grow.
  • Monthly allowance. OptiMonk, Poptin and Popupsmart. The counter resets each month, so the free tier can work indefinitely for a small site and fails abruptly for a busy one.
  • Trial. OptinMonster. Five hundred impressions, then a paid plan. Useful for an evaluation, not for running a site.
  • Feature-gated free. A generous free plan that withholds the one feature you need, usually exit intent, testing or analytics. The tier is technically free and practically not enough.

That last category is the one to watch. A cap is visible and easy to plan around; a missing trigger is not, and it tends to surface after the popup is built.

What none of them will do

No builder fixes a weak offer. A popup with a vague promise, a discount that is not worth typing an email for, or a form with five required fields will underperform in every tool on this page. The copy swipe file gives 24 patterns with placeholders, and the ROI calculator lets you test whether a popup is worth running at your traffic and value per signup. Neither makes a claim about typical results, because there is no honest typical result.

Two practical cautions apply to every tool here. First, popups can hurt if they block content on mobile or fire before the page is usable; test at phone widths. Second, consent rules apply to any popup that sets a cookie or loads a tracking pixel, and the builder does not decide that for you.

Common questions

What is the best free popup builder in 2026?

There is no single winner. Popup Maker is the strongest free option on WordPress because it is uncapped and self-hosted. PopupLoom is the strongest free option for a non-WordPress site that can add a script. OptiMonk has the largest free pageview allowance at 10,000 a month, and Poptin bundles email and coupons but caps the free plan at 1,000 unique visitors.

Which free popup builder has no pageview limit?

PopupLoom and Popup Maker do not meter pageviews, visitors or impressions. PopupLoom is a hosted script; Popup Maker is a self-hosted WordPress plugin. The other tools in this comparison set a monthly allowance that resets.

Is OptinMonster free?

No. OptinMonster's trial includes 500 campaign impressions and then auto-upgrades to a paid Plus plan. Its paid plans start at a promotional $108 for the first year on Basic, with Plus at $570 a year list. Treat it as a paid product with a short trial, not a free tier.

Which free plan is best for a Shopify store?

Popupsmart and Poptin are built with ecommerce campaigns in mind, including cart and coupon popups. Their free allowances are 5,000 pageviews and 1,000 unique visitors respectively, so a store with real traffic will outgrow either quickly. PopupLoom is cheaper but does not read a cart or generate coupon codes.

Do these tools count pageviews or visitors?

It varies. OptiMonk and Popupsmart meter pageviews, Poptin meters unique visitors, and OptinMonster meters campaign impressions. PopupLoom and Popup Maker do not meter traffic at all. Compare the unit before you compare the number.

Can I switch free popup builders later?

Yes, but there is no cross-vendor importer. Export submissions as CSV before you cancel, rebuild the live popup in the new tool, run both for a short overlap, then remove the old script. Keep the export as the historical record.

Are these rankings based on tests?

No. This is a documentation review of each vendor's public pricing and product pages, checked on 16 November 2026. No accounts were created, no campaigns were run and no conversion benchmarks are claimed. The comparison is grouped by fit, not by a single score.
