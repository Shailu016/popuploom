Free popup builder with no pageview limits

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 14 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 Vendor pricing rechecked 16 November 2026

The short answer. PopupLoom does not meter popup pageviews, unique visitors or impressions. In a pricing-page review of nine builders on 16 November 2026, only PopupLoom and Popup Maker's free WordPress plugin did not publish a traffic cap. Every other free tier had a meter: Poptin 1,000 visitors per month, Popupsmart 5,000 pageviews per month, OptiMonk 10,000 pageviews per month, Elfsight 200 widget views per month, OptinMonster a 500-impression trial, and Sleeknote and Wisepops trial-only plans.

The catch is scope. PopupLoom does not send email, run A/B tests, read carts or generate coupons. If you need those, a metered suite may be the better purchase. This page is a pricing-page review, not a hands-on trial: we did not create accounts on every tool or intentionally hit their caps.

Most "free" popup builders are metered. The free plan works until a pageview, visitor or impression cap is reached, and then the popup stops, the account auto-upgrades, or the widget is temporarily deactivated. The number in the advert is rarely the whole story; the unit, the reset period and what happens at the cap matter more.

This page explains those units, compares the free tiers we could verify from each vendor's own pricing page, shows what a cap means at a realistic traffic level, and says plainly when PopupLoom is the wrong choice.

What "no pageview limits" actually means

"Pageview", "unique visitor", "campaign impression" and "widget view" are different meters. A plan can look generous on one and expensive on another.

  • Pageview: every page load on your site. A visitor who reads ten pages counts as ten.
  • Unique visitor: one person or browser per month, however many pages they read. This is usually the kindest meter for a content site.
  • Campaign impression: every time the popup is shown. A sitewide popup can use one impression per visit even if the visitor reads several pages.
  • Widget view: every time the widget loads. This behaves like an impression, but the vendor may count a preview or a share link too.
  • Live popup or domain limit: a separate cap on how many campaigns or websites one free account can run. It can bind before the traffic meter does.
  • At the cap: some tools stop showing the popup, some auto-upgrade the plan, some charge overage, and some temporarily deactivate the widget.

PopupLoom has no pageview, visitor or impression meter for the popup itself. Hosting and API rate limits exist to stop abuse, not to bill you for traffic. The trade-off is scope: PopupLoom is a focused popup and lead-capture tool, not a full campaign suite.

The honest limits of "no limits"

"No pageview limit" is not the same as "unlimited everything". PopupLoom is deliberately narrow, and the gaps matter:

  • No built-in email or SMS sending, marketing automation or CRM. PopupLoom captures the response; your email tool delivers the follow-up.
  • No A/B testing, multi-step campaign builder, geotargeting or audience segmentation beyond page, device and frequency rules.
  • No cart contents, cart value, customer accounts or order history. A popup cannot read a cart it was not given.
  • No coupon generation, validation or syncing with a store. Create the code in your store and paste the real code into the popup.
  • No native iOS or Android SDK and no marketplace listing embed. It is a website widget.
  • No managed migration or enterprise support contract. Support is email-based and response times vary.
  • No guaranteed conversions, compliance or platform endorsement. You are responsible for the offer, consent language and claims you publish.

There are also fair-use limits. Public endpoints are rate-limited, and the service is designed for normal popup traffic rather than abuse. If you need a formal traffic SLA, a dedicated account manager or a custom data-processing agreement, this is not that product.

Switch without losing leads

Moving from a metered tool is mostly a lead-export and install-order exercise. Do not run both popups on the same page during the transition.

  1. Export the leads you already have from your current tool and store them with your records. PopupLoom has no automatic importer.
  2. Build the replacement popup in the PopupLoom studio. Test it as a guest first.
  3. Save it, open Publish, and copy the two-line install code.
  4. Add the code to your site and pause the old popup. Do not run both on the same page.
  5. Test desktop and mobile, submit one fake response, and confirm it appears in the lead inbox.
  6. Remove the old tool's code. Export PopupLoom leads as CSV whenever you need them.

There is no automatic lead import. Keep the old CSV; PopupLoom starts collecting new responses after installation. How to check any builder's limit yourself

Pricing pages are marketing pages. Read them like a contract, not a headline.

  1. Open the vendor's own pricing page, not a review site.
  2. Find the free tier and write down the unit: pageviews, visitors, impressions, views, contacts or live popups.
  3. Find the reset period: monthly, annual, per campaign or per domain.
  4. Find the "at the cap" behavior: stop, auto-upgrade, overage charge or temporary deactivation.
  5. Check whether the free tier is permanent or a trial. A 14-day trial is not a free plan.
  6. Check the other gates: domains, live popups, email quota, AI credits, branding and support.
  7. Record the date and the exact URL. Limits change, and a stale comparison is worse than none.

If a page says "unlimited" but the pricing table has a footnote, read the footnote. If a plan says "from" a price, assume the advertised price may be an annual or entry tier. If a free plan requires a card, treat it as a trial until you have confirmed otherwise.

Common questions

Is PopupLoom really free with no pageview limits?

Yes. PopupLoom does not meter pageviews, unique visitors or popup impressions. There is no trial countdown and no paid upgrade inside the product. A free account is required to save a popup and get its install code. Hosting and API rate limits protect the service from abuse.

Do I need a credit card?

No. You can design and preview as a guest, and create a free account without a card to save and install a popup.

What is the catch?

PopupLoom is deliberately narrow. It does not send email or SMS, run A/B tests, read cart data, generate coupons or provide a native app SDK. If you need those, a paid platform may fit better. The comparison above says who each tool is for.

Does "no pageview limit" mean unlimited everything?

No. There is no pageview, visitor or impression cap for the popup, but the service still has account, hosting and API rate limits to prevent abuse. It also has no email sending, A/B testing, geotargeting, cart data or coupon generation.

What happens when my traffic grows?

There is no popup traffic cap to upgrade. Hosting and API rate limits still apply, and a very large site should test the published page under its real traffic pattern. If you need a formal SLA or a dedicated account manager, PopupLoom is not that product.

Can I use it on WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace or Wix?

Yes where the platform allows custom HTML or JavaScript. PopupLoom is a two-line script, not a plugin. Platform-specific steps are in the WordPress, Shopify, Webflow and Squarespace/Wix guides. Some plans and checkout pages strip custom code, so test the published page.

Where are captured leads stored?

In your PopupLoom lead inbox, tied to the saved widget. You can export them as CSV. An optional signed server webhook can forward them to another tool.

Can I import leads from my old popup tool?

There is no automatic importer. Export the old leads as CSV and keep them with your records, then install PopupLoom for new responses.
