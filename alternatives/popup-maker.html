PopupLoom vs Popup Maker: a free alternative for any website

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 13 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 Popup Maker details checked 16 November 2026

The short answer, which may surprise you. If your site runs WordPress, Popup Maker's free plugin is uncapped, self-hosted and free. It is usually the better choice, and this page will not pretend otherwise. PopupLoom is for sites that cannot run a WordPress plugin: Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix, a custom build or anything where a plugin is the wrong tool.

The free plugin covers popups with auto-open, click and form-submission triggers. Exit intent, scroll triggers, analytics, advanced targeting, scheduling and split testing live in the paid Pro bundle, listed at $99 a year. This is a documentation review, not a hands-on trial: no Popup Maker account was created and the plugin was not installed.

Popup Maker is one of the oldest popup plugins in the WordPress directory. The listing shows it published in November 2014, last modified in September 2026 and currently at version 1.25.0, with more than 600,000 active installations and a 4.9/5 rating from 4,516 reviews. Its readme claims 780,000+ users, which counts everyone who has ever used it rather than current active installs; the two numbers measure different things and are not in conflict.

Being a plugin changes the economics. Popup Maker does not host your popups or meter your traffic, because the popup runs inside your WordPress installation and the data sits in your database. That removes the pageview question entirely, which is exactly the question that decides most of the comparisons on this site.

So the useful comparison is not “which is better” but “which platform are you on”. This page covers what the free plugin does, what the Pro bundle adds, what you own and owe when the data is in your database, and how to move in either direction.

What Popup Maker is

Popup Maker is a WordPress plugin for creating popups, slide-ins and announcement bars inside wp-admin. Its free version is a full popup builder, not a demo:

  • Popup types. Popups, slide-ins and announcement bars, with the standard WordPress editor for content and a theme customiser for styling.
  • Free triggers. Auto-open on page load, click, and form submission. Those three cover a welcome popup, a manual “show offer” button and a thank-you popup.
  • Conditions. Page, post type and user-role conditions are part of the free plugin, along with display limits and cookies.
  • Unlimited traffic. No pageview or impression cap, because the plugin runs on your server.
  • Data locality. Submissions and settings stay in your WordPress database, under your backups and retention rules.

The free plugin is GPL software from Daniel Iser and the Popup Maker team, maintained in the WordPress.org directory. That matters for two reasons: you can audit the code, and you can keep it running without a vendor account.

Where Popup Maker is the better choice

For a WordPress site, most of the arguments favour the plugin:

  • No traffic meter. Self-hosted means no pageview, visitor or impression allowance to track, and no upgrade path tied to traffic.
  • No third-party script. Pages do not load a popup vendor's JavaScript, which avoids a class of caching, consent and performance conflicts.
  • Data in your database. Submissions are yours, subject to your own backups and privacy policy. Nothing is exported to a vendor.
  • Deep WordPress integration. User roles, post types and the WordPress editor are native to the plugin, so targeting rules read the way WordPress reads.
  • Free is genuinely usable. Auto-open, click and form-submission triggers cover a lot of small-site campaigns at zero cost.
  • Auditable and portable. GPL code, no account required to run it, and the plugin can be removed without asking a vendor for an export.

If you are on WordPress and your popup needs are a welcome offer, a newsletter prompt or a click-to-reveal code, the free plugin is hard to beat. PopupLoom would add a third-party dependency for no gain.

Where PopupLoom fits instead

PopupLoom exists for the sites that the plugin cannot reach. A Shopify theme, a Webflow project, a Squarespace page, a Wix site or a hand-built application cannot install a WordPress plugin. Those sites can usually add a script, which is what PopupLoom is.

  • Any platform that allows a script. Two lines before the closing body tag: a widget element with the saved widget id and a script tag.
  • No meter and no paid tier. No pageviews, visitors, impressions or credits. Hosting and API rate limits protect the service but are not a plan boundary.
  • Hosted configuration. Edits go live from the studio without re-pasting the snippet, which helps when the site is managed by someone who does not deploy code.
  • Portable leads. A lead inbox, CSV export and an optional signed webhook with HMAC-SHA256, retries and a delivery log.
  • A small, honest feature set. 45 templates, six triggers, page and device targeting, frequency rules, countdowns, copyable codes and form fields.

The costs are just as real: no exit-intent trigger, no scroll trigger, no analytics, no split testing and no WordPress admin integration. PopupLoom also cannot match the plugin's data locality, because the configuration and responses are hosted. If those matter more than platform coverage, stay on WordPress with Popup Maker.

What you take on with a plugin

Self-hosting is not free of cost; it moves the cost from a subscription to maintenance. A few things are worth knowing before you install:

  • Script conflicts. Caching and optimisation plugins that defer or combine JavaScript can stop a popup from appearing. The usual fix is to exclude the popup's scripts from deferral, then purge the cache.
  • Consent and cookies. A popup that sets a cookie or loads a tracking pixel is in scope for consent rules. The plugin cannot decide that for you, and the free version does not manage consent on your behalf.
  • Database growth. Every submission is a row in your database. A high-traffic popup can add thousands of rows a month, which affects backups and exports over time.
  • Updates and testing. Plugin updates can change markup. A popup that worked before an update needs a quick check on the published page, not just in the preview.

None of that is a reason to avoid the plugin on WordPress. It is a reason to plan for it. If you would rather not maintain a plugin, the WordPress without a plugin guide covers the script route, and the popup not showing guide covers the common caching and conflict cases.

How to evaluate the free plugin in ten minutes

  • Check the trigger list. If you need exit intent or scroll, the free plugin does not have it and Pro is the real comparison.
  • Test with your cache on. Publish a popup, open the page in a private window and confirm it appears with caching and optimisation active.
  • Check where entries go. Find the entries screen and export a test row before you build a campaign around it.
  • Read the renewal terms. The first-year price is a promotion; the renewal is the number that matters for a multi-year site.

Moving in either direction

From Popup Maker to PopupLoom

  1. Export submissions from the plugin's entries screen before deactivating anything.
  2. Note the offer, pages, trigger and frequency of each live popup.
  3. Rebuild in the studio and replace every placeholder.
  4. Add the two-line snippet through the theme or a code-snippet plugin.
  5. Disable the plugin popup, then verify in a private window.

From PopupLoom to Popup Maker

  1. Export your leads as CSV and import them into your email provider.
  2. Install the free plugin from the WordPress directory.
  3. Recreate the popup and its conditions in wp-admin.
  4. Add Pro if you need exit intent, scroll, analytics or split testing.
  5. Remove the PopupLoom snippet from the theme.

Neither direction is automatic. Popup Maker keeps data in the WordPress database, so an export is a database export rather than a vendor handover; PopupLoom keeps data in its lead inbox and exports CSV. In both cases, keep the old export until the new popup has run a full business cycle.

Common questions

Is PopupLoom a free Popup Maker alternative?

Only outside WordPress. For a WordPress site, Popup Maker's free plugin is uncapped and native, and it is usually the better choice. PopupLoom is for Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace, Wix and custom sites that can add a script but cannot run a WordPress plugin.

Does Popup Maker limit pageviews or impressions?

No. Popup Maker's own material describes unlimited pageviews and impressions on the free plugin and the paid bundles. It is self-hosted WordPress software, so there is no vendor-side traffic meter to hit.

What does Popup Maker Pro add?

The Pro bundle adds extensions that are not in the free plugin, including exit-intent triggers, scroll triggers, popup analytics, advanced targeting conditions, scheduling and split testing. The free plugin covers auto-open, click and form-submission triggers.

How much does Popup Maker Pro cost?

At the time of checking, the pricing page listed Pro at $99 a year and Pro+ at $249 a year, with a 40% first-year discount showing $59.40 and $149.40. Prices and promotions change, so confirm at checkout.

Can I use Popup Maker on Shopify or Webflow?

No. Popup Maker is a WordPress plugin and requires a WordPress installation. Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace and Wix do not run WordPress plugins, so those sites need a script-based builder instead.

Where do Popup Maker leads go?

Submissions are stored in your WordPress database and can be connected to an email provider through integrations. You own the database, but you also own its backups and retention.

Is PopupLoom a WordPress plugin?

No. PopupLoom is a hosted script that you add to a page before the closing body tag. It works on WordPress if the theme or a code-snippet plugin allows custom JavaScript, but it is not installed through the plugin directory.

Is PopupLoom affiliated with Popup Maker?

No. Popup Maker is a trademark of its owner. PopupLoom is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Popup Maker, and no Popup Maker account was created for this review.
