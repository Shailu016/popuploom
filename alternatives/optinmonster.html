OptinMonster alternative: what the free trial really includes

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 14 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 OptinMonster terms checked 16 November 2026

The short answer. OptinMonster's free offer is a trial with 500 campaign impression credits, not a permanent free plan. When those credits run out, the account auto-upgrades to the paid Plus plan. Paid Basic starts at a displayed $9/month equivalent on a first-year promotion and includes 2,500 campaign impressions per month for one site.

PopupLoom has no impression, pageview or visitor meter and no trial timer. It is also much narrower: no A/B testing, no geotargeting, no revenue attribution, no 700-template library and no managed support. If those features pay for themselves, stay with OptinMonster. If your popup is a lead-capture or announcement widget and the impression meter is the only thing you think about, the switch is worth costing out.

OptinMonster is a mature campaign platform operated by Retyp, LLC. Its pricing page advertises more than 700 templates, campaign types that include popups, fullscreen takeovers, floating bars, slide-ins and inline forms, plus A/B testing, geotargeting, revenue attribution and a WordPress plugin with more than a million active installations on WordPress.org. None of that is in dispute here.

The confusion is about the free tier. “Try OptinMonster for free” is not the same as “OptinMonster is free”. The trial carries an impression allowance and an automatic upgrade path. That matters most to small sites, because 500 impressions can disappear in a day on a sitewide popup, and the upgrade is a paid subscription, not a warning screen.

This page is a pricing-page review, not a hands-on OptinMonster trial. We did not create an OptinMonster account or run its editor. The screenshots below are from the live PopupLoom build and show the migration path. The OptinMonster facts are dated, quoted and linked so you can recheck them before you buy or cancel.

What the 500-impression trial actually gives you

The pricing page, checked on 16 November 2026, says the trial “includes all the features of Basic with just 500 impression credits” and “auto-upgrades to Plus when impressions are exceeded.” That sentence contains the three facts that matter:

  • It is a trial. There is no permanent free plan to stay on indefinitely.
  • The unit is a campaign impression. Not a pageview, not a unique visitor and not an email contact.
  • The default exit is a paid Plus plan. The trial does not simply stop when the credits run out.

An impression is one display of the campaign. If a sitewide popup is configured to show once per visit, 500 impressions is roughly 500 visits. If it is configured to show on every pageview, a visitor who reads four pages can consume four credits. OptinMonster's own frequency settings decide which behavior applies; the point is that you should calculate impressions from your traffic pattern, not from your monthly pageview count.

The trial also mirrors Basic, not the higher tiers. On the same pricing page, A/B testing is listed under Plus, Exit Intent technology is listed under Pro, and geolocation, OnSite Retargeting and follow-up campaigns are listed under Growth. A trial can therefore tell you whether the editor and basic campaigns fit your workflow, but it cannot validate the advanced features you may actually be buying.

One more commercial detail: OptinMonster says new plans are billed annually by default and renew automatically. Monthly plans exist, but the annual price is presented as roughly half the monthly equivalent. The advertised first-year rates on 16 November 2026 were a Black Friday promotion, and the pricing page states that renewals are at full price. Budget for the renewal, not the promotional headline.

Where OptinMonster is genuinely stronger

A fair comparison has to start with the features the cheaper tool does not have. These are not minor omissions; they are the reason many teams pay for a suite.

  • Template depth. The pricing page advertises more than 700 templates. PopupLoom has 45. More choice is useful if you need a specific seasonal, ecommerce or industry layout and do not want to design from scratch.
  • Campaign formats. OptinMonster lists popups, fullscreen welcome mats, floating bars, slide-ins, inline forms, scroll boxes, coupon wheels and yes/no forms. PopupLoom offers modal, fullscreen, four slide-in corners, a top bar and a bottom bar. OptinMonster covers more formats, especially inline and embedded campaigns.
  • Testing and targeting. A/B testing is listed from Plus, device targeting and content locking from Plus, and geolocation, new-versus-returning targeting, OnSite Retargeting and follow-up campaigns from Growth. PopupLoom has page, device and frequency rules but none of those.
  • Revenue and attribution. Pro lists revenue attribution, countdown timers, Smart Tags, referrer and query targeting, campaign scheduling and analytics integrations. PopupLoom has no revenue reporting and no cart data.
  • Exit intent. Exit Intent technology is listed under Pro on the 16 November 2026 page. PopupLoom includes a desktop exit-intent trigger in the free product, which is a real advantage for a small site that only wants that one behavior.
  • WordPress and support. OptinMonster has a native WordPress plugin with more than 1 million active installations on WordPress.org and advertises human support. PopupLoom is a script with email support and no plugin.
  • Operational scale. OptinMonster offers multiple sites, sub-accounts, integrations and a custom plan above Growth. PopupLoom is a single-owner account model with no agency or enterprise layer.

If you recognize your workflow in that list, the honest recommendation is to stay. Switching to a cheaper tool to avoid a $210 annual renewal, then rebuilding the same workflow in three other products, is not a saving.

Where PopupLoom fits instead

PopupLoom is deliberately narrow. The case for it is not “same product, lower price”. The case is that a large share of small sites only need a well-designed popup, a few targeting rules and a place for the responses to land.

  • No traffic meter. There is no pageview, visitor or impression cap for the popup, no trial countdown and no paid upgrade inside the product.
  • One focused workspace. 45 templates, modal/fullscreen/slide-in/bar placements, six triggers (page load, timer, scroll depth, scroll past an element, click and desktop exit intent), page-path rules, device switches and frequency caps.
  • Responses you can take with you. A lead inbox, CSV export and an optional signed server webhook with retries and a delivery log.
  • A two-line install. Save the popup, copy the snippet and paste it before the closing body tag. There is no plugin to maintain and no theme-level lock-in.
  • Free account, no card. Guests can design and preview; an account is required to save and install.

The trade-off is equally clear: PopupLoom does not send email or SMS, does not run A/B tests, does not read carts or revenue, does not generate coupons, does not offer geotargeting and does not provide managed support. It is a popup and lead-capture tool, not a conversion suite.

Who should switch, and who should stay

Switch if…

  • You run one small site and a single popup job: email signup, a discount, an announcement or a lead magnet.
  • You do not use A/B testing, geotargeting, revenue attribution or multi-step campaigns.
  • The impression allowance is the constraint you actually feel, not the feature list.
  • You want CSV ownership and a two-line script instead of a plugin and a dashboard.
  • You are comfortable handling email follow-up in your own email tool.

Stay if…

  • You rely on A/B testing, content locking or multi-view campaigns.
  • You need exit intent plus revenue attribution on the same plan.
  • You use geolocation, retargeting or follow-up campaigns for ecommerce.
  • You want a native WordPress plugin, sub-accounts and human support.
  • You need more than 100,000 impressions or a custom contract.
  • The suite already produces measurable revenue that covers its renewal.

The worst reason to switch is a promotional price. The second worst is a feature checklist you will never use. Cost the workflow you actually run: the popups, the triggers, the place leads land and the time it takes to maintain them.

What a 10,000-impression month costs

Suppose your site displays a sitewide popup about 10,000 times in a month. On OptinMonster, Basic includes 2,500 impressions, so the account moves up a tier. Plus includes 10,000 impressions and was displayed at $288 for the first year, renewing at $570/year. If your popup displays 25,000 times, Pro was displayed at $348 for the first year, renewing at $870/year.

PopupLoom charges $0 for the same popup displays, but it does not include the suite features that justify the OptinMonster price. The comparison is only meaningful if you subtract what you would otherwise pay for testing, targeting, analytics and support. If you need none of those, the saving is real. If you need half of them, the saving is a false economy.

Also separate impressions from traffic. If the popup shows once per visit, 10,000 impressions is roughly 10,000 visits, which may be far more traffic than 10,000 pageviews. If it shows on every pageview, the same 10,000 impressions can come from a much smaller audience. Use your analytics to estimate displays, not sessions, before comparing plans.

How to migrate without losing leads

The migration is mostly an export-and-install exercise. Do not run both popups on the same page during the transition; two overlapping offers annoy visitors and make the results impossible to read.

  1. Export your existing leads. Use the export option in your OptinMonster account and keep the CSV. If your plan does not expose an export, ask support before you cancel. PopupLoom has no automatic importer.
  2. Inventory the campaigns you actually use. Note the page targeting, trigger, frequency and offer for each one. Rebuild only the campaigns that earn their place.
  3. Build the replacement as a guest. Open the PopupLoom studio, choose a template and test the content, trigger and mobile layout without saving.
  4. Save and publish. Create the free account, save the popup, open Publish and copy the two-line install code.
  5. Pause the old campaign, then install the new one. Add the snippet before the closing body tag, publish, and submit one fake response. Confirm it appears in the lead inbox.
  6. Watch for a week before cancelling. Keep the old CSV and the old account long enough to confirm the new popup fires on the pages you expect. Then remove the old code and cancel.

Platform-specific placement is covered in the WordPress, WordPress without a plugin, Shopify, Webflow and Squarespace and Wix guides. The installation guide covers the exact snippet and the testing sequence.

What you give up with PopupLoom

“No pageview limit” is a traffic statement, not a feature statement. The gaps are real and worth listing before you migrate:

  • No A/B testing or multi-step campaigns. You can change a popup, but you cannot split traffic between two versions inside the product.
  • No geotargeting, retargeting or audience segmentation beyond page paths, device type and frequency.
  • No cart, order or revenue data. A popup cannot react to a cart it was never given.
  • No coupon generation or validation. Create the code in your store and paste the real code into the popup.
  • No email or SMS sending. The response is captured; your email provider handles delivery and automation.
  • No native WordPress plugin, sub-accounts or managed support. It is a script and an email support channel.
  • Fair-use limits. Public endpoints are rate-limited to prevent abuse. There is no traffic quota, but there is also no enterprise SLA.

If any of those gaps is a must-have, the honest answer is to keep the suite that has it. The point of this page is not that free is always better; it is that “free trial” and “free plan” are different offers, and the difference should be visible before you commit.

How to evaluate OptinMonster yourself

  1. Open the vendor's pricing page, not a review site or an affiliate listicle.
  2. Write down the trial unit and the exit: how many credits, and what happens when they run out.
  3. Estimate impressions from your analytics and your frequency setting, not from monthly pageviews alone.
  4. Check which tier contains the features you need: A/B testing, exit intent, geotargeting, revenue attribution.
  5. Check the billing cadence, the renewal price and whether the displayed price is promotional.
  6. Check the site count and what happens if you exceed the impression allowance.
  7. Read the money-back guarantee terms and the cancellation process before you enter a card.
  8. Confirm you can export your leads before you need to leave.

If the answers still point to OptinMonster, buy it with a clear budget for the renewal. If they point to a narrower tool, compare the no-limits alternatives and the free-builder shortlist before you move.

Common questions

Is PopupLoom a free OptinMonster alternative?

Yes, with a narrower scope. PopupLoom is free and does not meter pageviews, visitors or popup impressions. It does not include OptinMonster's A/B testing, geotargeting, revenue attribution, 700+ template library or managed support.

What does OptinMonster's free trial include?

OptinMonster's pricing page on 16 November 2026 says the free trial includes all Basic features with 500 impression credits, and auto-upgrades to the paid Plus plan when impressions are exceeded. It is a trial, not a permanent free plan.

How many impressions does OptinMonster Basic include?

The Basic plan listed 2,500 campaign impressions per month for one site on 16 November 2026. Plus listed 10,000 impressions, Pro 25,000 and Growth 100,000. Verify current limits before buying.

Does OptinMonster include exit-intent popups?

On the pricing page checked on 16 November 2026, Exit Intent technology was listed under the Pro plan, not Basic or Plus. The free trial includes Basic features, so check the current feature list before relying on exit intent during a trial.

Is OptinMonster billed monthly or annually?

OptinMonster says new plans are billed annually by default and renew automatically. Monthly plans are available at a higher effective price. The first-year promotional rates shown on 16 November 2026 renew at the published full annual price.

Can I migrate my OptinMonster leads to PopupLoom?

There is no automatic importer. Export the leads from your OptinMonster account and keep the CSV with your records. PopupLoom collects new responses in its own lead inbox after installation.

Does PopupLoom send email or SMS?

No. PopupLoom captures form responses, stores them in the lead inbox, exports them as CSV and can forward new leads to a signed server webhook. Email and SMS delivery remain with your own provider.
