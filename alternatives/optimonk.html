PopupLoom vs OptiMonk: a free alternative without pageview limits

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 13 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 OptiMonk pricing checked 16 November 2026

The short answer. OptiMonk's free plan allows 10,000 pageviews per month, 1,000 AI credits and one domain, with unlimited campaigns and no branding. Paid plans start at $228 billed annually for 20,000 pageviews and rise to $2,148 for 500,000. Above one million pageviews the price is quoted.

That is the most generous free pageview allowance of the popup tools we priced in November 2026. PopupLoom still differs in kind: no pageview meter, no AI credits, no paid tier, and a much smaller feature set. This is a pricing-page review, not a hands-on trial: no OptiMonk account was created and its editor was not run.

OptiMonk is an established conversion-optimisation platform: popups, gamification, quizzes, upsells and analytics, now with an AI layer that can draft and adjust campaigns. Its pricing page says it serves 30,000 brands. That claim is the vendor's own and we did not audit it; it is worth knowing because it explains the product's shape. OptiMonk is built for marketers running a programme, not for a site owner who wants one email popup.

PopupLoom is the opposite kind of tool. It is a free widget with 45 templates and no traffic meter, and it stops at the captured response. Neither product is a worse version of the other; they are sized for different jobs. This page sets out the plans, the two limits OptiMonk meters, and the cases where switching makes sense and where it does not.

What OptiMonk is, and who it is built for

The product spans more than popups:

  • Campaign types. Email popups, exit-intent popups, gamification, upsell popups, quiz popups and seasonal offers are each presented as a use case with its own templates and playbook.
  • AI assistance. An AI layer drafts copy and can act through an agent. OptiMonk publishes an MCP tool reference, which means the AI assistants that support MCP, such as Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor, can operate the account through a documented interface.
  • Analytics and testing. Campaign reporting and A/B testing are part of the platform rather than an add-on.
  • Integrations. An integration library for email, ecommerce and CRM tools, plus popup templates in a public library.

That breadth is why OptiMonk competes for ecommerce and agency work. A store can run a quiz for product matching, an upsell popup at checkout, a seasonal campaign and an exit-intent offer, then compare results in one dashboard. An agency can hand the account to an AI assistant and ask it to build variants.

PopupLoom does none of that. It is a popup and lead-capture widget: 45 templates, six triggers, page and device targeting, frequency rules, a lead inbox, CSV export and an optional signed webhook. The honest comparison is between a campaign platform with a free tier and a single free widget.

The free plan is the headline. Ten thousand pageviews is twice Popupsmart's free allowance and, unlike many competitors, OptiMonk does not restrict campaigns or features on it. A small store can run a welcome popup, an exit-intent popup and a quiz at the same time without paying, provided the pageviews stay under the cap.

The AI credits are the part that needs care. OptiMonk meters them separately from pageviews, and the pricing page does not publish the credit cost of an individual AI action. That matters because the free tier's real ceiling may be reached through AI use rather than traffic. If you plan to lean on the AI agent, ask support what a typical campaign draft or optimisation run costs before you treat 1,000 credits as unlimited.

Two meters, not one

Most popup tools meter exactly one thing. OptiMonk meters three: pageviews, AI credits and domains. The first two reset monthly; the third is a hard cap on how many sites the plan can serve.

  • Pageviews. Every page load counts. A visitor who reads five articles counts five times. On a content site with long sessions, 10,000 pageviews can arrive from fewer than 2,500 readers.
  • AI credits. Consumed by AI drafting, optimisation and agent actions. The free plan includes 1,000 a month; the paid plans include 3,000, 10,000 and 20,000. The per-action cost is not on the pricing page.
  • Domains. One on free, two on Essential, four on Growth, ten on Premium. A second brand needs at least Essential even if both sites are quiet.

PopupLoom meters none of these. There is no pageview, visitor or impression limit, no AI allowance because there is no AI, and no domain count in the product. Hosting and API rate limits exist to protect the service, but they are not a plan boundary you can outgrow into a paid tier.

The practical consequence: with OptiMonk, the question is not “will the free plan work?” but “which of the three limits will I hit first?” For a single small site that barely uses AI, pageviews bind. For an agency testing AI-generated variants, credits bind. For a two-brand owner, domains bind.

Where OptiMonk is stronger than PopupLoom

  • AI and agents. OptiMonk can draft copy and operate through MCP-aware assistants. PopupLoom has no AI features and no API for an agent to drive.
  • Campaign breadth. Quizzes, gamification, upsells, seasonal campaigns and exit intent, each with templates and reporting. PopupLoom has 45 templates across fewer formats and no quiz or upsell logic.
  • Testing and analytics. A/B testing and campaign analytics are built in. PopupLoom offers neither.
  • Integrations. A broad email, ecommerce and CRM library. PopupLoom exports CSV or posts a signed webhook; the wiring is yours.
  • Free-tier capacity. 10,000 pageviews, unlimited campaigns and no branding is a stronger free offer than most competitors, including Popupsmart's 5,000-pageview, one-campaign tier and Poptin's 1,000-visitor tier.
  • Support at the top end. Premium includes a key account manager, and Master is a custom arrangement above a million pageviews.

If any of those is central to your plan, stay with OptiMonk. The free tier alone may be enough for a small store, and that is a fair reason not to switch. The case for PopupLoom starts when the meter, not the feature list, is the problem.

Where PopupLoom fits

  • No meter of any kind. No pageviews, visitors, impressions, credits or domains to track.
  • Free with no card. Guests can design and preview; a free account is needed to save and get the install code.
  • Two-line install with hosted configuration. Edits go live without re-pasting the snippet.
  • Portable leads. Lead inbox, CSV export and an optional signed webhook with HMAC-SHA256, retries and a delivery log.
  • Small surface area. 45 templates, six triggers, page and device targeting, frequency rules, countdowns, copyable codes and form fields. Nothing to configure that you do not need.

The trade-offs are equally plain: no AI, no quizzes or upsells, no A/B testing, no analytics dashboard, no integrations and no team workflow. PopupLoom also needs a site that allows custom HTML or JavaScript; the installation guide covers that, and the WordPress without a plugin guide covers blocked-script cases.

Arithmetic: displayed annual price ÷ (monthly pageviews ÷ 1,000). The 50,000-visitor row costs more per thousand than the 20,000 row because the next plan up doubles the allowance to 100,000. That is not an error in the table; it is how stepped allowances work. Verify current prices and credit allowances before buying.

The step pattern is the useful finding. OptiMonk's plans jump from 20,000 to 100,000 pageviews, so a site at 25,000–30,000 pageviews pays for nearly four times the capacity it needs. At 50,000 the effective rate is worse than at 20,000. If your traffic sits between the tiers, the arithmetic favours buying less elsewhere or negotiating a custom plan rather than accepting the default step.

Who should switch, and who should stay

Switch to PopupLoom if…

  • You want one or two popups, not a tested campaign programme.
  • Your pageviews are approaching 10,000 a month and you would rather not track a meter.
  • You do not use the AI, quiz or upsell features that justify OptiMonk's price.
  • You already have an email provider and only need the captured response.

Stay with OptiMonk if…

  • You run several campaign types and want them reported together.
  • You use AI drafting or an MCP agent in your workflow.
  • You need A/B testing, integrations or multiple domains.
  • You are under 10,000 pageviews and the free tier already fits.

How to migrate from OptiMonk in about half an hour

  1. Export your submissions. Keep the CSV and confirm any connected email provider has the historical contacts.
  2. List the live campaigns. Note offer, pages, trigger, frequency and which ones actually earned their place.
  3. Rebuild the best performer first. Open the studio, pick a template from the catalog and replace every placeholder.
  4. Set one trigger and one frequency rule. Do not copy a quiz's settings onto a discount popup.
  5. Test as a guest at desktop and phone widths. The exit-intent guide covers the desktop-only trigger.
  6. Publish and install the two-line snippet. Platform notes: WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Squarespace and Wix.
  7. Pause OptiMonk and verify. Check that the response lands in the lead inbox before you remove the old code.
  8. Cancel only after a full business cycle. Keep the OptiMonk export; PopupLoom does not import it.

The collect and export leads guide covers inbox, CSV and webhook routing. Expect quiz, upsell and gamification campaigns not to carry over: PopupLoom does not reproduce that logic.

How to evaluate OptiMonk's free plan yourself

  • Measure pageviews, not users. Compare your analytics pageview count with 10,000, and remember long sessions count more.
  • Estimate AI usage. Ask support what an AI draft or agent action costs in credits before planning around 1,000.
  • Count domains. One brand fits the free tier; two need Essential.
  • Confirm the at-limit behaviour. The pricing page does not say whether a campaign pauses or an overage is billed.

Common questions

Is PopupLoom a free OptiMonk alternative?

Yes, with a narrower scope. PopupLoom is free and does not meter pageviews or AI credits. It does not include OptiMonk's AI assistant, A/B testing, gamified and upsell campaign library, analytics dashboard or integration set.

What is OptiMonk's free plan?

On 16 November 2026 OptiMonk's pricing page listed a free plan with 10,000 pageviews per month, 1,000 AI credits and one domain, with unlimited campaigns and no branding.

What is an AI credit on OptiMonk?

The pricing page lists AI credits as a separate monthly allowance but does not publish the credit cost of each AI action there. The free plan includes 1,000 credits; higher plans include 3,000, 10,000 and 20,000. Ask support what one generation or agent action costs before you plan around the free allowance.

Does OptiMonk count pageviews or visitors?

OptiMonk's plans are listed in pageviews per month, so each page load counts toward the allowance. That is a stricter meter than a unique-visitor limit on a site where readers view several pages per session.

Is OptiMonk's free plan really free?

The page presents it as a free plan with no stated expiry and no branding requirement. As with any free tier, the allowance resets monthly and the at-limit behaviour is not spelled out on the pricing page, so confirm the terms before relying on it for a launch.

Does PopupLoom have AI features like OptiMonk?

No. PopupLoom has no AI copy generation, AI targeting or agent integration. It provides 45 templates, six triggers, targeting rules, a lead inbox, CSV export and a signed webhook.

Can I move my OptiMonk popups to PopupLoom?

There is no automatic importer. Export your OptiMonk submissions or copy them into your email provider, rebuild the live popup in PopupLoom, and keep the export as the historical record. New responses land in the PopupLoom lead inbox.
