Poptin alternative: a free popup builder without visitor limits

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 14 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 Poptin pricing checked 16 November 2026

The short answer. Poptin's free-forever plan is metered at 1,000 unique visitors per month on one domain. Paid plans move to 10,000 visitors ($240 billed annually for Basic), 50,000 ($564 for Pro) and 150,000 ($1,140 for Agency). The visitor count resets monthly.

PopupLoom has no visitor, pageview or impression meter and no paid tier. It is also narrower: no email sending, A/B testing, cart reading, coupon generation or native app SDK. If you want popups and email in one dashboard, Poptin's free plan may be the better starting point even with the cap. This is a pricing-page review, not a hands-on trial: we did not create a Poptin account or run its editor.

Poptin is a popup, form and email-marketing platform. Its pricing page presents one interface for popups, forms, coupons and email contacts, aimed at small businesses, ecommerce stores and agencies. That combination is the reason people shortlist it: one login, one contact list and one place to build a campaign.

The trade-off is the meter. Poptin's free plan is generous in features but small in traffic. On a site with 5,000 monthly visitors, the free allowance is consumed five times over. That does not make Poptin expensive; it makes the free tier trial-sized for a growing site. The question is whether the paid plan buys something you would otherwise pay for separately, such as email marketing.

This page lays out the current plans, the unit Poptin counts, the features you get and give up, and the migration path. Prices and limits are dated and linked so you can recheck them before you buy.

What Poptin is, and who it is built for

Poptin's own pricing page describes a product with four connected parts:

  • Popups and forms. A drag-and-drop editor with lightbox popups, announcement bars, slide-ins, fullscreen overlays, gamified and countdown popups, coupon popups, mobile and video popups, surveys, teasers and product-recommendation popups. The comparison table on its pricing page lists 60+ popup templates.
  • Coupons. Fixed and unique codes, coupon creation and sync for Shopify and Wix, bulk upload, tracking, dynamic delivery, expiry and targeting.
  • Email. A separate email plan with active contacts, campaigns, segments and automations. The free email tier lists 250 active contacts and 500 emails.
  • Rules and integrations. Exit-intent, delay, scroll, page count, click, inactivity, cart and autopilot triggers; page, device, schedule, browser, traffic-source, geolocation and cookie targeting; an API and a long integration list.

That is a broad product: a store can build a first-order popup, collect the address into Poptin's contact list, send a welcome email and track a coupon in one dashboard. An agency can manage client subaccounts. A small site can use the free plan on one domain and never touch a paid tier if it stays under 1,000 visitors.

PopupLoom is deliberately not that product. It is a popup and lead-capture widget: 45 templates, six triggers, page and device targeting, frequency rules, a lead inbox, CSV export and an optional signed webhook. It does not send email, generate coupons or read a cart. The comparison only makes sense if you separate the popup from the marketing suite around it.

What the free plan actually includes

This is a more capable free tier than many popup tools offer. On the free plan, Poptin lists unlimited popups and forms, one domain, 1,000 unique visitors per month, API access, and chat and email support. The visible exclusions on the plan card are unbranded popups, subaccounts and team-user management.

The practical meaning:

  • You can build more than one popup. The free card says unlimited popups and forms on one domain; the “1 live popup” limit belongs to Popupsmart, not Poptin.
  • You cannot remove Poptin's credit or manage client subaccounts. Branding needs Basic; agency workflow needs Agency.
  • You are capped at 1,000 unique visitors per month. That is the number to watch, and a single campaign on a busy page can cross it in days.

What happens at the cap is the part we did not test. Poptin's pricing page presents the paid tiers as the next step; it does not promise a grace period or an overage rule in the material we checked. If you plan to run near the limit, ask support what happens when the 1,000th visitor arrives, and whether the overage is blocked, billed or simply counted.

Where Poptin is stronger than PopupLoom

A fair comparison starts with what the other product does better. Poptin wins on breadth and on the work that happens after the form submit.

  • Email marketing in the same account. Contacts, segments, campaigns and automations are part of Poptin's product. PopupLoom stops at the captured response.
  • Coupons and ecommerce actions. Poptin lists fixed and unique codes, Shopify and Wix sync, bulk upload and expiry. PopupLoom does not generate or validate coupon codes.
  • More formats. Surveys, teasers, product recommendations, video, gamified popups and announcement bars, with 60+ templates. PopupLoom has 45 templates and fewer layouts.
  • More targeting rules. Cart triggers, inactivity, page count, traffic source, cookie and geolocation targeting. PopupLoom has six triggers, page include/exclude, device switches and frequency rules.
  • Agency workflow and integrations. Subaccounts, team users, premium support and a broad integration list.

If you need any of those, stay with Poptin or a comparable suite. A free popup tool that forces you to rebuild your email, coupon and reporting stack elsewhere is not cheaper once your time is counted.

Where PopupLoom fits

PopupLoom's advantage is not feature count. It is that the popup has no traffic meter and the data stays portable. For a site that already has email, a store platform and analytics, the popup is a small part of the stack; paying a suite price mainly to remove a visitor cap can be the wrong purchase.

  • No cap. No pageview, visitor or impression meter for the popup. Hosting and API rate limits protect the service, but there is no plan to upgrade when traffic grows.
  • Free with no card. No trial timer and no paid upgrade inside the product. A free account is required to save a popup and get its install code; guests can design and preview.
  • Two-line install. A widget element with the saved widget id and a script tag. The configuration is hosted, so edits go live without re-pasting the snippet.
  • Portable leads. A lead inbox, CSV export and an optional signed server webhook with HMAC-SHA256, retries and a delivery log. No contacts product is required.
  • Useful constraints. 45 templates, six triggers, page and device targeting, frequency rules, countdowns, copyable codes and form fields. Enough for a focused popup, not a campaign suite.

The trade-off is real: no email sending, no A/B testing, no coupon generation, no cart reading and no native app SDK. PopupLoom also cannot escape an iframe or run on a platform that blocks custom scripts. The installation guide covers the platform requirement, and the WordPress without a plugin guide covers the most common blocked-script cases.

Who should switch, and who should stay

Switch to PopupLoom if…

  • You already use a separate email provider and do not need Poptin's contacts or campaigns.
  • Your traffic is near or above 1,000 monthly visitors and the cap is the main reason you are considering a paid plan.
  • You want a simple script, a hosted configuration and no plugin dependency.
  • You are comfortable exporting leads or forwarding them to a webhook.
  • Your popup is a focused lead-capture, announcement, discount or exit-intent widget, not a full campaign program.

Stay with Poptin if…

  • You want popups, forms and email contacts in one dashboard.
  • You rely on coupon generation, Shopify/Wix coupon sync or cart triggers.
  • You need agency subaccounts, team users or premium support.
  • You use Poptin's integrations as part of an existing workflow.
  • You are under 1,000 monthly visitors and the free plan already does everything you need.

How to migrate from Poptin in about half an hour

The migration is mostly bookkeeping. PopupLoom does not import Poptin's historical contacts, so keep the export and treat the switch as a clean start for new responses.

  1. Export your Poptin contacts and submissions. Keep the CSV where your team can reach it. If you use Poptin's email features, choose the provider that will send the follow-up before you cancel anything.
  2. List the popups that are live. Note the offer, page, trigger, frequency and whether each one collects an email address or just announces something.
  3. Rebuild the most important popup first. Open the PopupLoom studio, pick a template from the catalog and replace every placeholder with a real offer, code and date.
  4. Set the behavior. Choose one trigger, one placement and a frequency rule. A welcome popup and an exit-intent popup should not share the same settings.
  5. Test as a guest. Submit a fake response, close and reopen the popup, and check desktop and phone widths. The exit-intent guide covers the desktop-only trigger.
  6. Save, publish and install. Copy the two-line snippet from the publish panel and add it before the closing body tag. Platform notes: WordPress, Shopify, Webflow and Squarespace and Wix.
  7. Pause Poptin, then verify. Disable the old popup, load the page in a private window and confirm the response lands in the lead inbox.
  8. Remove the old script. Once the new popup has run for a full business cycle, delete the Poptin code and cancel the plan if you no longer need its email or coupon features.

If you want the lead-routing details before you start, the collect and export leads guide explains the inbox, CSV and webhook options. If you are moving a WordPress site that blocks scripts, read the WordPress without a plugin guide first.

How to evaluate Poptin's free plan yourself

Pricing pages are marketing pages. Read them like a contract, then verify the details in the product.

  • Find the unit and the reset. Poptin counts unique visitors and resets monthly. Confirm your analytics reports the same unit, and check what happens if you cross the limit mid-month.
  • Find the at-cap behavior. Ask support whether the popup stops, the account is throttled or an overage is charged. Do not assume a warning screen.
  • Check branding and contacts. The free plan carries a Poptin credit. Poptin says contacts and visitors scale together, so a plan that fits your traffic may not fit your email list.
  • Check the commitment. The displayed monthly figure assumes annual billing. Compare it with monthly pricing, cancellation terms and the export path if you leave.

Common questions

Is PopupLoom a free Poptin alternative?

Yes, with a narrower scope. PopupLoom is free and does not meter unique visitors, pageviews or popup impressions. It does not include Poptin's email marketing, coupon tools, cart triggers or agency account management.

What is Poptin's free plan?

Poptin's pricing page on 16 November 2026 listed a free-forever plan with 1,000 unique visitors per month, one domain, unlimited popups and forms, API access, and chat and email support. The popups carry Poptin branding on the free plan.

Does Poptin count pageviews or unique visitors?

Poptin's pricing page says visitors are counted as unique visitors per month and the visitor count resets monthly. A visitor who reads ten pages counts once for the popup meter, not ten times.

What happens when I reach Poptin's 1,000-visitor free limit?

The pricing page directs accounts that need more traffic to a paid plan: Basic lists 10,000 visitors per month, Pro 50,000 and Agency 150,000. We did not test Poptin's exact at-cap behaviour, so verify it in the product before relying on the free tier.

Does PopupLoom include email sending like Poptin?

No. PopupLoom captures the response, stores it in the lead inbox, exports it as CSV and can forward it to a signed server webhook. It does not send marketing email, so follow-up stays with your email provider.

Can I migrate my Poptin leads to PopupLoom?

Export your Poptin contacts or submissions and keep the CSV. PopupLoom has no automatic importer; it starts collecting new responses after you install the widget. Historical leads stay in the file you exported.

Can I use PopupLoom on Shopify or WordPress?

Yes where the theme, app or page builder allows custom HTML or JavaScript. Shopify theme code does not cover checkout, and WordPress caching or security plugins can block a third-party script. Test on the published page before switching.
