Popup copy generator

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 16 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026

What this does. Pick an industry, a goal and a tone, and it assembles twelve popup copy sets: badge, headline, subheadline, button and disclaimer. Every combination is deterministic, so the same choices always produce the same text.

What it does not do. It does not know your offer, your audience or your results. These are structural patterns with placeholders, not tested winners, and no conversion rate is implied. Replace [X], [DATE] and [CODE] before publishing.

Most popup copy fails for one of three reasons: the offer is vague, the button does not say what happens next, or the small print contradicts the headline. The generator is built around those failure modes. The headline carries one idea, the button names the action, and the disclaimer stays literal.

It deliberately avoids the language of certainty. No “last chance” unless there is a real deadline, no “only 3 left” unless stock is actually counted, and no “exclusive” for a code anyone can find. Copy that cannot be honoured at checkout costs more than it earns.

Keeping the copy honest

  • One idea per popup. A discount and a newsletter and a survey in one overlay is three asks, and visitors answer none of them.
  • The button names the outcome. “Send my code” is clearer than “Submit”. The visitor should know what happens before they click.
  • The disclaimer matches the offer. If the code is one-use, say so. If the waitlist has no date, do not imply one.
  • No fake scarcity. A countdown that resets on reload teaches visitors to distrust every timer on the site.
  • Write for the smallest screen. If the headline does not fit on a phone, it is too long for a popup.

    • Test one change at a time

    A popup has several variables: the trigger, the placement, the offer, the headline, the button and the frequency. If you change three at once, you learn nothing about which one mattered. Pick one element, run it for a full business cycle, and compare the response count against the previous period at similar traffic.

    This tool generates copy, not a test plan. It cannot tell you whether your audience prefers urgency to friendliness, and it does not pretend to. The ecommerce playbook covers trigger and placement choices that interact with the copy.

    Common questions

    Are these popup copy examples tested winners?

    No. They are structural patterns assembled from the goal and tone you choose. They have not been tested on your traffic and no conversion rate is claimed. Run your own comparison before treating any of them as better than what you have.

    What do the placeholders mean?

    [X] is the discount or value, [DATE] is the expiry or deadline, and [CODE] is the coupon code. Replace every placeholder before publishing; an unresolved bracket is the most common mistake in copied popup text.

    Can I use this copy for email or ads?

    The patterns are written for popups, but the structure transfers. Check the claims against what you can actually deliver, and keep any deadline or discount literal. A popup that promises something the checkout cannot honour is worse than no popup.

    Why does the generator not show conversion rates?

    Because there is no honest universal rate. Results depend on the offer, the audience, the traffic source, the trigger and the page. The tool generates copy, not forecasts, and it collects no data.

    Does the generator send my inputs anywhere?

    No. Everything runs in your browser. There are no network calls, no analytics and no storage of the text you enter.

    How do I put this copy into a popup?

    Open the studio, pick a template, and paste the badge, headline, subheadline and button text into the content fields. The disclaimer can go in the small print under the form. Then set a trigger and frequency before publishing.
