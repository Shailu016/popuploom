Popup ROI calculator

By Shailu, founder of PopupLoom Published 16 November 2026 Last updated 16 November 2026 Runs entirely in your browser

This calculator turns your own traffic, conversion and value assumptions into monthly signups, annual value, break-even numbers and free-tier cap pressure. Every field is editable. Nothing is submitted to PopupLoom or to any third party.

It is deliberately small. It does not tell you what a good conversion rate is, because the honest answer depends on your offer, audience, page, device, traffic source and what you count as a conversion. PopupLoom publishes no benchmark conversion rate and no average subscriber value. The defaults below are placeholders so the page produces output before you type; replace every one with a number you can defend.

Read this before using the result. The output is arithmetic, not a forecast. It assumes the traffic and conversion rate you enter stay flat for twelve months, that every signup has the same value, and that the popup has no effect on other conversions. Real sites rarely behave that way.

Use it to compare scenarios and to find the assumptions that matter. Do not use it to promise a return, budget a media spend or claim a guaranteed lift.

Every output comes from the inputs above. There is no hidden model, no historical data and no industry multiplier.

Where to get each input

Monthly visitors

Use the unique-visitor figure from your analytics for a representative 30-day period. Exclude internal traffic, bots and test visits if your analytics allows it. If your traffic is seasonal, run the calculator for a slow month and a strong month instead of averaging them into one number. A single annualised result hides the months that decide whether the tool is affordable.

Popup displays per visitor

Displays are not visitors. A popup that can render on every pageview can display several times to the same person. A once-per-day frequency rule with a single daily visit is closer to one display per visitor. If your popup tool reports impressions, use the last 30 days of impressions divided by the same period's visitors. If you are planning a new popup, state the frequency rule you intend to use and estimate from it.

Conversion rate

Use a measured submit rate for a comparable popup, on a comparable page, with a comparable offer. Do not borrow a rate from a different industry, a different traffic source or a different incentive. If you have no measurement yet, treat the calculator as a planning tool with a range of rates — for example, your low, middle and high guesses — rather than picking one number and believing it. The 1% default is a placeholder, not a recommendation.

Value per signup or lead

This is the number most likely to be wrong. A newsletter signup is not worth the same as a demo request, and a discount signup is not worth the gross order value. Use contribution margin after the discount, refunds and delivery costs. If you cannot defend a value, enter 0 and use the calculator only for the traffic and cap sections until you can.

Popup tool cost

Use the amount you actually pay, including the annual plan divided by twelve if that is how you are billed. If you are comparing a free tool with a paid one, run the calculation twice: once with the paid tool's real cost and once with 0. Then decide whether the paid tool's extra features are worth the difference, not whether free is always better.

Free-tier cap

The presets show the shape of a cap rather than a live price list: a small monthly impression allowance, a one-time trial credit, a visitor allowance and two pageview allowances. They are representative of the free and entry tiers vendors published in November 2026, not a claim about any particular plan. A cap can be monthly, one-time or measured in a unit you do not track. Match the unit to your analytics before you trust the comparison.

What the calculator leaves out

A positive result is not a business case until you account for the costs and effects outside the form. At minimum, consider:

  • Email or SMS delivery. Capturing a lead is not the same as following up. Your email provider, SMS credits and automation tool may cost more than the popup tool.
  • Discount margin. A 10% discount reduces the value of the order. If the popup gives away margin, the value per signup should be net of that giveaway.
  • Refunds, chargebacks and fraud. A lead or order that later reverses is not worth the same as one that sticks.
  • Cannibalisation. Some visitors who would have subscribed anyway may use the popup. The incremental signups can be lower than the raw count.
  • Repeat displays and annoyance. The formula treats every display as equally valuable. Visitors who see the same popup repeatedly may ignore it or leave.
  • Design and maintenance time. Building, testing and updating the popup is work. The calculator does not price your time.
  • Consent and privacy obligations. The lawful basis for contacting a lead, the privacy notice and any consent language are your responsibility. The calculator does not model them.
  • Seasonality and traffic quality. A conversion rate from a warm email click is not the same as a cold search visit. Annualising one month can hide both.

How to use the result without fooling yourself

Do this

  • Run at least three scenarios: a low, middle and high conversion rate.
  • Change one input at a time and note which one moves the result most.
  • Compare the result with your actual lead count after the popup has run for a full period.
  • Use net value, not gross revenue, when the offer carries a discount.
  • Keep the date and the source of every number you enter.

Avoid this

  • Treating the output as a forecast or a guarantee.
  • Using a conversion rate from a different offer, audience or traffic source.
  • Counting every signup as revenue when the value arrives months later, if at all.
  • Ignoring the email tool, design time and discount cost.
  • Annualising one unusually good week and calling it a plan.

Common questions

What counts as a popup display?

A display is one rendering of the popup on a page. It is the unit many vendors meter as an impression. If a popup can appear on every pageview and a visitor reads four pages, that visitor can produce four displays.

Should I enter visitors or pageviews?

The main traffic field is monthly visitors. A separate pageviews-per-visitor field lets the calculator compare your usage with a pageview cap. If you only track pageviews, divide by your average pageviews per visitor before entering the visitor number.

What conversion rate should I enter?

Enter a rate you measured for a popup or a closely comparable offer, and keep the measurement period consistent with the traffic period. PopupLoom does not publish a benchmark rate, and the example value in the form is a placeholder, not a recommendation.

What is value per signup?

It is what one captured signup or lead is worth to your business over the period you are measuring. Use your own margin, repeat-purchase rate or sales follow-up data. Do not use gross revenue if the offer carries a discount or refund cost.

How is the break-even conversion rate calculated?

The calculator divides the monthly tool cost by the product of monthly displays and value per signup, then multiplies by 100. That is the conversion rate at which the estimated monthly value exactly covers the monthly tool cost.

Does the calculator include email, SMS or design costs?

No. It only includes the monthly tool cost you enter. Email delivery, SMS credits, design time, discount margin, refunds, chargebacks and staff time are not modelled. Add those costs separately before treating a positive result as profit.

Does the calculator predict revenue or guarantee a return?

No. It performs arithmetic on the numbers you supply. It does not forecast traffic, conversion rates or revenue, and it does not account for seasonality, audience quality or offer fit. Treat every output as a scenario, not a promise.

Does PopupLoom meter pageviews or popup impressions?

No. PopupLoom does not meter pageviews, visitors or popup impressions for the widget. Public endpoints apply rate limits to prevent abuse, but there is no traffic cap or trial countdown. Hosting and fair-use limits still apply.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. The calculator runs in your browser and does not submit the values to PopupLoom or any third party. Reloading the page resets the fields to their example values.

Method and limitations

This page is a client-side calculator, not a research study. The formulas are the ones shown in the table above. The cap presets are illustrative allowances drawn from the kinds of free and entry tiers published in November 2026; they are not tied to a named plan and may not match any current offer. We did not run a traffic test through any vendor's meter or verify every plan's current terms. Prices, units and plan names can change.

No ranking, traffic, review, certification or conversion claim is made on this page. PopupLoom does not guarantee that a popup will produce signups, revenue or a positive return. The calculator is provided for planning and comparison; you are responsible for the accuracy of the numbers you enter and for the privacy and consent rules that apply to your business.
